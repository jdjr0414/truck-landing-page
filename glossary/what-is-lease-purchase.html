What Is Lease-Purchase?

Lease-purchase (or lease-to-own) is a lease agreement with an option to buy the equipment at the end of the term. You make lease payments during the term; at the end, you can purchase the equipment for a predetermined price (often $1 or nominal). Common for garbage trucks, fire apparatus, street sweepers, and other municipal or fleet vehicles.

Key Takeaways

  • Lease-purchase = lease with option to buy at end
  • Common for municipal and fleet equipment
  • May offer lower payments than a loan; ownership at end
  • Purchase price at end often nominal ($1)

Why Use Lease-Purchase

Municipalities and fleets often use lease-purchase for budgeting (spread cost over time), flexibility (option to return if needs change), and sometimes favorable terms from manufacturers or municipal finance programs.

Lease-Purchase vs Operating Lease

Lease-purchase builds equity toward ownership; you typically buy at the end for $1 or nominal. An operating lease does not include a purchase option; you return the equipment at term end. Lease-purchase is common for garbage trucks, fire apparatus, and street sweepers. See Lease vs Loan Truck Financing.

Typical Terms

Lease-purchase terms typically run 48–84 months for commercial vehicles. Payments may be lower than a loan because the lessor retains title during the term. At the end, the purchase option is usually exercised for nominal consideration. Budget approval may be required for municipal entities.

Common Questions

Is lease-purchase the same as lease-to-own?

Yes. Lease-purchase and lease-to-own are the same structure–lease with option to buy at the end.

Can I get lease-purchase for a dump truck?

Yes. Lease-purchase is available for dump trucks, garbage trucks, and other vocational equipment. Some lenders and manufacturers offer these programs.

What happens if I don't buy at the end of a lease-purchase?

You typically have the option to return the equipment. Terms vary by agreement. Some contracts require purchase; read the agreement carefully.
