Financing for Bucket Trucks & Aerial Work Platforms

Bucket truck financing covers aerial work platforms mounted on truck chassis. Used by utilities, tree care, telecom, electrical contractors, and municipal crews. Bucket trucks combine a commercial chassis with an aerial device—lenders finance the complete unit. Both new and used bucket trucks are widely financed.

Typical Financing Scenarios

Financing terms vary by borrower profile. Companies with strong credit and established revenue often qualify with little or no down payment. Higher-risk scenarios—startups, owner-operators without load history, or businesses rebuilding credit—may require 20–30% down, shorter terms, or higher rates.

  • Established trucking companies: Fleets with 2+ years in business typically receive the best terms—often 10–15% down or less.
  • Owner-operators: May qualify with carrier agreements or load history. Down payments of 15–25% are common.
  • Startups: Often need 20–30% down, a business plan, and proof of contracts.
  • Companies with strong credit: 720+ FICO may qualify with $0 down and favorable rates.
  • Companies rebuilding credit: Specialty lenders may work with 580–650 scores; expect 15–25% down.

Who Needs Bucket Truck Financing?

Bucket truck financing serves utility contractors, tree care companies, telecom installers, electrical contractors, and municipal departments. These businesses need aerial access for line work, tree trimming, antenna installation, and maintenance. Bucket trucks range from 35-foot to 65-foot+ working height. Lenders evaluate the combined value of chassis and aerial device.

Aerial Specs and Valuation

Bucket truck value depends on working height, platform capacity, and chassis. A 55-foot over-center aerial has different value than a 35-foot rear-mount. Lenders familiar with utility equipment understand these specs. Document the aerial manufacturer, model, and reach. Well-maintained aerials retain value—inspect boom condition and hydraulic systems before purchase.

New vs. Used Bucket Truck Financing

New bucket trucks qualify for 60–84 month terms and 10–15% down for qualified borrowers. Used bucket truck financing typically runs 36–60 months with 20–30% down. Aerial condition matters—lenders may require inspection reports for older units. Telecom bucket truck financing covers units configured for communications work; terms are similar.

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Licensing requirements for operating a bucket truck vary by state, vehicle weight, business activity, and cargo type. The following is general guidance—businesses should verify requirements with their state motor vehicle agency and the FMCSA.

Licensing Quick Answer

Bucket trucks typically require a Class B CDL due to weight (26,000+ GVWR). OSHA safety training is often required for aerial work. DOT registration may apply for interstate use.

Driver License Requirements

Commercial vehicles are regulated by weight (GVWR—gross vehicle weight rating) and configuration. Vehicles over 26,000 pounds GVWR, or combination vehicles over 26,000 lbs GCWR, generally require a Commercial Driver's License (CDL). Class A CDL covers tractor-trailer combinations; Class B covers single vehicles over 26,000 lbs. Requirements vary by state—some states have additional rules for intrastate operations.

Weight-Based Licensing Thresholds

Federal CDL requirements apply to vehicles with a GVWR of 26,001 pounds or more, or combination vehicles with a GCWR of 26,001 pounds or more. Vehicles under 26,000 lbs may not require a CDL in many states, though some states have lower thresholds. Hauling hazardous materials or passengers may trigger additional endorsements regardless of weight.

Typical Experience or Training Expectations

Many industries require training or operating experience beyond the CDL:

  • CDL training: Commercial driver training schools offer CDL preparation. Some employers provide in-house training.
  • Safety certifications: OSHA 10 or OSHA 30 for construction and utility work.
  • Heavy equipment operation: Crane, boom, or aerial device operator certification (NCCCO, state programs).
  • Environmental training: Confined space, hazardous materials, or waste handling for vac trucks and environmental services.
  • Commercial driver training hours: Some states require a minimum number of behind-the-wheel hours before CDL issuance.

Can You Operate This Vehicle Without a CDL?

Bucket trucks typically exceed 26,000 pounds GVWR and require a Class B CDL. OSHA training is often required for aerial work regardless of license class.

Common Questions

Do you need a CDL to drive a bucket truck?

Bucket trucks typically require a Class B CDL due to weight (26,000+ GVWR). OSHA safety training is often required for aerial work. DOT registration may apply for interstate use.

Do operators need special training for bucket truck?

CDL training is required. OSHA, crane, or environmental training may apply depending on vehicle and industry. Employer-specific certifications are often expected.

What class CDL is required for a bucket truck?

Often Class B CDL depending on weight. Utility operation. Requirements vary by state and vehicle configuration.

Do you need a DOT number for a bucket truck?

DOT registration is typically required for interstate commerce. Intrastate operations depend on state regulations. Verify with the FMCSA and your state agency.

How long does it take to get licensed for a bucket truck?

CDL training programs typically run 2–8 weeks. State testing and endorsement processing may add time. Endorsements (tanker, hazmat) require additional testing.

Can a startup business operate a bucket truck?

Yes. Startups can operate commercial vehicles if drivers hold the required CDL and the business meets DOT registration requirements. Financing may require proof of contracts or revenue.

What credit score is needed to finance a bucket truck?

Most lenders prefer 600+ for competitive rates. 720+ typically qualifies for the best terms. Utility contractors with steady contract revenue may qualify with lower scores.

How much down payment is required for bucket truck financing?

Typically 10–30%. New bucket trucks often allow 10–15%; used may require 20–30%. Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with little or no down payment.

Can startups finance bucket trucks?

Yes. Some lenders work with newer utility contractors. Expect 20–30% down, proof of contracts, and strong personal credit. Utility vendor status helps.

How long do bucket truck loans usually last?

New bucket trucks: 60–84 months. Used: 36–60 months depending on age and aerial condition. Aerial reach and specs affect terms.

How quickly can bucket truck financing be approved?

Pre-approval: 24–72 hours. Full approval and funding: typically 1–5 business days. Have business documentation and aerial specs ready.

Can I finance a used bucket truck?

Yes. Used bucket truck financing is widely available. Terms are typically 36–60 months. Aerial condition and hydraulic systems affect valuation.

What documents are needed for bucket truck financing?

Business tax returns (2 years), bank statements (3–6 months), driver's license, and equipment details (chassis and aerial specs, make, model, price).

How much does a bucket truck typically cost?

New bucket trucks: $90,000–$250,000. Used: $40,000–$120,000. Cost depends on aerial reach (35–65+ feet). See how much does a bucket truck cost.

What interest rates can I expect for bucket truck financing?

Rates typically range from 7% to 15% APR. Prime credit may qualify for 7–10%. Used equipment often carries rates 1–3% higher than new.
