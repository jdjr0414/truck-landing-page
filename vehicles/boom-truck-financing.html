Definition

A boom truck is a commercial vehicle with a truck-mounted crane or material-handling boom for lifting and placing materials. Boom trucks are similar to crane trucks and are used in construction, precast delivery, utilities, and logistics. They differ from bucket trucks, which provide personnel access rather than material handling. Crane operators may need NCCCO certification depending on the job.

Key Facts About Boom Trucks

  • Typical cost: $80k – $250k
  • Common industries: construction, logistics
  • License often required: Class B CDL
  • Typical financing terms: 48–72 months

Who Needs Boom Truck Financing?

Construction contractors, utility contractors, and material handling operations. Boom trucks lift precast, steel, equipment, and materials. Revenue comes from project work, contracts, or per-lift fees. Lenders evaluate business revenue, time in business, and equipment value.

Boom Specs and Valuation

Boom truck value depends on crane capacity (tonnage), reach, and chassis. Knuckle-boom and articulated cranes are common. Document crane manufacturer, model, capacity, and chassis for accurate valuation.

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Licensing requirements for operating a boom truck vary by state, vehicle weight, business activity, and cargo type. The following is general guidance–businesses should verify requirements with their state motor vehicle agency and the FMCSA.

Driver License Requirements

Commercial vehicles are regulated by weight (GVWR–gross vehicle weight rating) and configuration. Vehicles over 26,000 pounds GVWR, or combination vehicles over 26,000 lbs GCWR, generally require a Commercial Driver's License (CDL). Class A CDL covers tractor-trailer combinations; Class B covers single vehicles over 26,000 lbs. Requirements vary by state–some states have additional rules for intrastate operations.

DOT Registration Requirements

Businesses that operate commercial motor vehicles in interstate commerce must register with the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) and obtain a USDOT number. Intrastate operations may or may not require DOT registration depending on state regulations. Requirements vary by state, vehicle weight, and type of operation.

Weight-Based Licensing Thresholds

Federal CDL requirements apply to vehicles with a GVWR of 26,001 pounds or more, or combination vehicles with a GCWR of 26,001 pounds or more. Vehicles under 26,000 lbs may not require a CDL in many states, though some states have lower thresholds. Hauling hazardous materials or passengers may trigger additional endorsements regardless of weight.

Typical Experience or Training Expectations

Many industries require training or operating experience beyond the CDL:

  • CDL training: Commercial driver training schools offer CDL preparation. Some employers provide in-house training.
  • Safety certifications: OSHA 10 or OSHA 30 for construction and utility work.
  • Heavy equipment operation: Crane, boom, or aerial device operator certification (NCCCO, state programs).
  • Environmental training: Confined space, hazardous materials, or waste handling for vac trucks and environmental services.
  • Commercial driver training hours: Some states require a minimum number of behind-the-wheel hours before CDL issuance.

Can You Operate This Vehicle Without a CDL?

Boom trucks typically exceed 26,000 pounds GVWR and require a Class B CDL. Operator certification for the boom/crane is often required.

Common Questions

Do you need a CDL to drive a boom truck?

Boom trucks typically require a Class B CDL. Crane or material handler certification may be required. DOT registration for commercial operations.

Do operators need special training for boom truck?

CDL training is required. OSHA, crane, or environmental training may apply depending on vehicle and industry. Employer-specific certifications are often expected.

What class CDL is required for a boom truck?

Often Class B CDL. 26,000+ GVWR. Requirements vary by state and vehicle configuration.

Do you need a DOT number for a boom truck?

DOT registration is typically required for interstate commerce. Intrastate operations depend on state regulations. Verify with the FMCSA and your state agency.

How long does it take to get licensed for a boom truck?

CDL training programs typically run 2–8 weeks. State testing and endorsement processing may add time. Endorsements (tanker, hazmat) require additional testing.

Can a startup business operate a boom truck?

Yes. Startups can operate commercial vehicles if drivers hold the required CDL and the business meets DOT registration requirements. Financing may require proof of contracts or revenue.

What credit score is needed to finance a boom truck?

Most lenders prefer 600+ for competitive rates. 720+ typically qualifies for the best terms. Construction and utility contractors with project revenue may qualify with lower scores.

How much down payment is required for boom truck financing?

Typically 10–30%. New boom trucks often allow 10–15%; used may require 20–30%. Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with little or no down payment.

Can startups finance boom trucks?

Yes. Some lenders work with newer construction or utility contractors. Expect 20–30% down, proof of contracts, and strong personal credit.

How long do boom truck loans usually last?

New boom trucks: 60–84 months. Used: 36–60 months depending on age and crane condition. Crane capacity and reach affect terms.

How quickly can boom truck financing be approved?

Pre-approval: 24–72 hours. Full approval and funding: typically 1–5 business days. Have business documentation and crane specs ready.

Can I finance a used boom truck?

Yes. Used boom truck financing is widely available. Terms are typically 36–60 months. Crane condition and capacity affect valuation.

What documents are needed for boom truck financing?

Business tax returns (2 years), bank statements (3–6 months), driver's license, and equipment details (crane capacity, chassis, price).

How much does a boom truck cost to finance?

Boom trucks range from $70,000 to $220,000+ depending on crane capacity. Down payments typically run 10–30%. Used for precast, steel, and material handling.

What is a boom truck?

A boom truck has a truck-mounted crane or material handler for lifting and placing materials. Used in construction and logistics. Boom trucks and crane trucks are similar; crane trucks may have higher capacity.
