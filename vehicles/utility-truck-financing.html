Types of Utility Trucks

Utility trucks include bucket trucks for aerial line work, service trucks with service bodies and tool storage, vac trucks for hydro excavation (utility locate work), and crane trucks for material handling. Telecom bucket trucks are configured for communications work. Utility fleets often mix these truck types.

Who Needs Utility Truck Financing?

Electric utility contractors, telecom installers, tree care companies, municipal electric departments, and infrastructure maintenance firms. Revenue comes from utility contracts, line work, tree trimming, or telecom installation. Lenders evaluate contract revenue, time in business, and equipment value. Utility contractors often have steady contract revenue that supports financing.

New vs. Used Utility Truck Financing

New utility trucks qualify for 60–84 month terms and 10–15% down. Used utility truck financing typically runs 36–60 months with 20–30% down. Bucket trucks and specialized bodies retain value when well-maintained. Lenders familiar with utility equipment offer appropriate programs.

What Lenders Evaluate

  • Contract revenue: Utility contracts, municipal work, or telecom agreements.
  • Time in business: 12–24 months minimum; 2+ years for stronger terms.
  • Equipment: Chassis, body type, aerial or crane specs, and condition.
  • Credit: Personal and business credit affect rate and approval.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a utility truck?

A utility truck is a work truck used by utility contractors—often a bucket truck, service truck, or truck with specialized body for line work.

Can I finance a bucket truck?

Yes. Bucket trucks are commonly financed as utility trucks. See bucket truck financing for aerial-specific details.

What down payment is needed for utility trucks?

Typically 10–30%. New units often allow 10–15%; used may require 20–30%.

Who uses utility trucks?

Electric utilities, telecom contractors, tree care companies, and municipal departments. Utility trucks support line work and infrastructure maintenance.
