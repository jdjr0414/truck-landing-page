Refrigerated (Reefer) Truck Financing

Last Updated: March 2026

Financing for Refrigerated Trucks, Reefer Trailers & Reefer Units

Refrigerated truck financing covers temperature-controlled equipment used to move perishable freight—reefer box trucks, refrigerated trailers, and the refrigeration units themselves. Reefers keep produce, frozen food, meat, dairy, floral, and pharmaceutical cargo within a controlled temperature range from pickup to delivery. Because a reefer combines a truck or trailer with a costly refrigeration unit, financing often bundles both into a single loan.

Key Takeaways

  • The reefer unit alone can add $15k–$30k and can be financed separately
  • Financing terms commonly range from 36–84 months depending on age and unit hours
  • Strong credit businesses may qualify with little or no down payment

Definition

A refrigerated truck (reefer) is a truck or trailer fitted with a self-contained refrigeration unit that holds cargo at a set temperature. Reefers move perishable freight—produce, frozen goods, meat, seafood, dairy, flowers, and pharmaceuticals. The two most common forms are the reefer box truck (a straight truck with an insulated cargo box and a nose-mounted refrigeration unit) and the refrigerated trailer pulled by a semi tractor. The refrigeration unit itself, most often built by Thermo King or Carrier Transicold, is a major cost component and is tracked by run hours the way an engine is tracked by mileage.

What Reefer Financing Covers

Refrigerated truck financing is broader than a single vehicle loan because a reefer is really two assets working together: the truck or trailer, and the refrigeration unit bolted to it. Lenders finance these in three common ways.

  • Reefer box trucks – A straight truck with an insulated cargo body and a nose-mounted refrigeration unit. The whole vehicle is financed as one collateralized asset, similar to a standard box truck but with the reefer included in the value.
  • Refrigerated trailers – A reefer trailer pulled by a separate semi tractor. Because the trailer is a distinct asset, it is usually financed apart from the tractor. See semi trailer financing and semi truck financing for the tractor side.
  • Reefer units on their own – When a refrigeration unit wears out, the replacement can be financed as standalone equipment on an existing chassis. This keeps a serviceable truck or trailer in revenue while spreading the unit cost over time.

Bundling the truck and unit into one loan is the most common structure for new purchases. Standalone unit financing is most useful mid-life, when the chassis has years left but the original refrigeration unit is at the end of its service life.

Rates and Terms

Rates for refrigerated truck financing typically range from 7% to 16% APR for qualified commercial borrowers. Because a reefer carries a wear component (the refrigeration unit) on top of the truck, terms are frequently structured a notch more conservatively than for dry equipment of the same age.

New vs. Used Reefer Financing

New reefers qualify for the longest terms (60–84 months), the lowest rates, and the highest advance rates. Manufacturers and their finance arms sometimes offer promotional programs on new dealer purchases. Used reefer financing is more nuanced than used dry equipment because the refrigeration unit ages on its own clock.

Reefer unit hours matter. A refrigeration unit logs run hours whenever it is cooling, independent of how many miles the truck has driven. A trailer with modest mileage can still carry a unit with 15,000–20,000+ run hours, which signals major service or replacement ahead. Lenders and buyers evaluate unit hours the way they evaluate engine mileage: high hours mean shorter terms, higher rates, or a larger down payment. Always ask for the unit's hour reading and service history alongside the truck's odometer.

Maintenance and Downtime Notes

A reefer earns nothing when its unit is down, and a temperature failure can spoil an entire load. Underwriters know this, so a business's maintenance discipline can influence approval and structure. Key points operators and lenders watch:

  • Unit service intervals: Refrigeration units need scheduled maintenance measured in run hours, not just calendar time. Budget for pretrip inspections, belt and filter service, and periodic major service.
  • Downtime cost: A failed unit can idle the truck and put a contract at risk. Some operators keep a maintenance reserve or run newer units on temperature-sensitive lanes.
  • Refrigerant and compliance: Older units may use phased-down refrigerants; verify the refrigerant type on used equipment, as it affects future service cost.
  • Monitoring: Telematics and temperature logging protect cargo and provide proof of the cold chain, which matters for produce and especially pharmaceutical loads.

What Lenders Evaluate

  • Time in business: Most prefer 12–24 months minimum; stronger programs want 2+ years.
  • Revenue: Cold-chain contracts, produce hauling agreements, or route revenue that supports the payment. Owner-operators may provide load history.
  • Credit: Personal and business credit affect rate and approval. Scores of 650+ typically qualify for competitive terms.
  • The reefer unit: Age, run hours, make (Thermo King, Carrier), and service history—evaluated separately from the truck or trailer.
  • Equipment: Truck mileage or trailer age, box insulation condition, and resale value in the reefer market.

Typical Financing Scenarios

Financing terms vary by borrower profile. Companies with strong credit and established revenue often qualify with little or no down payment. Higher-risk scenarios—startups, owner-operators without load history, or high-hour used reefers—may require 20–30% down, shorter terms, or higher rates.

  • Established cold-chain carriers: 2+ years in business often qualify for favorable terms—typically 10–15% down or less.
  • Owner-operators: May qualify with produce or frozen contracts and load history. Down payments of 15–25% are common.
  • Startups: Often need 20–30% down, a business plan, and proof of contracts.
  • Companies with strong credit: 720+ FICO may qualify with $0 down on new reefers.
  • Companies rebuilding credit: Specialty lenders may work with 580–650 scores; expect 15–25% down and closer scrutiny of unit hours.

Financing the Reefer Unit Itself

When the truck or trailer is sound but the refrigeration unit is worn out, replacing the unit alone is often the smarter move than buying a whole new reefer. A new self-contained unit runs roughly $15,000–$30,000 installed and can be financed as standalone equipment, typically over 24–48 months. This keeps a paid-off or serviceable chassis generating revenue while the unit cost is spread over time. Standalone unit loans are also common when upgrading to a newer, more efficient unit for a temperature-sensitive contract.

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Licensing requirements for operating a refrigerated truck vary by state, vehicle weight, business activity, and cargo type. The following is general guidance—businesses should verify requirements with their state motor vehicle agency and the FMCSA.

Driver License Requirements

Commercial vehicles are regulated by weight (GVWR—gross vehicle weight rating) and configuration. Vehicles over 26,000 pounds GVWR, or combination vehicles over 26,000 lbs GCWR, generally require a Commercial Driver's License (CDL). A reefer trailer pulled by a tractor requires a Class A CDL; a heavier reefer box truck typically requires a Class B CDL, while some smaller reefer box trucks under 26,000 lbs may not require a CDL in certain states. Requirements vary by state.

Businesses that operate commercial motor vehicles in interstate commerce must register with the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) and obtain a USDOT number. Weight-Based Licensing Thresholds

Federal CDL requirements apply to vehicles with a GVWR of 26,001 pounds or more, or combination vehicles with a GCWR of 26,001 pounds or more. Reefer box trucks under 26,000 lbs may not require a CDL in many states, though some states have lower thresholds. Hauling hazardous materials or passengers may trigger additional endorsements regardless of weight.

Typical Experience or Training Expectations

Many cold-chain operations expect training or experience beyond the CDL:

  • CDL training: Commercial driver training schools offer CDL preparation. Some employers provide in-house training.
  • Reefer unit operation: Familiarity with Thermo King or Carrier unit controls, setpoints, and pretrip checks.
  • Food-safety awareness: Handling and temperature-control practices for FDA and USDA cargo.
  • Cold-chain documentation: Logging temperatures and maintaining records for sensitive loads.
  • Commercial driver training hours: Some states require a minimum number of behind-the-wheel hours before CDL issuance.

Can You Operate This Vehicle Without a CDL?

Sometimes. A small reefer box truck under 26,000 pounds GVWR may not require a CDL in certain states. A reefer trailer pulled by a tractor always requires a Class A CDL, and heavier reefer box trucks typically require a Class B CDL. Check your vehicle's GVWR and configuration.

Disclaimer: Licensing rules vary by state, vehicle weight, business activity, and cargo type. Requirements change over time. Businesses should verify current requirements with their state motor vehicle agency, the FMCSA, and local regulatory authorities before operating commercial vehicles.

Common Questions

How much does a reefer box truck cost?

Refrigerated box trucks typically cost $60,000–$120,000 new depending on cargo length, GVWR, and the reefer unit installed. Used reefer box trucks often run $35,000–$70,000. See how much does a box truck cost for related pricing.

How much does a reefer trailer cost?

New refrigerated trailers typically cost $50,000–$90,000. Used reefer trailers commonly range from $25,000–$55,000 depending on age, reefer unit hours, and condition.

What does it cost to replace a reefer unit?

A new self-contained refrigeration unit (Thermo King or Carrier) typically costs $15,000–$30,000 installed. Used units run less. The unit can be financed separately or bundled into a truck or trailer loan.

Can I finance the reefer unit itself?

Yes. The refrigeration unit can be financed as part of the truck or trailer, or as a standalone equipment loan (typically 24–48 months) when replacing a worn-out unit on an existing chassis.

What rates and terms apply to refrigerated truck financing?

Rates typically range from 7% to 16% APR for qualified commercial borrowers. Terms run 60–84 months for new equipment and 36–60 months for used reefers, depending on age and unit hours.

Do reefer unit hours matter when financing a used reefer?

Yes. Lenders weigh reefer unit run hours alongside truck mileage. High unit hours signal upcoming maintenance and can shorten terms, raise rates, or increase the down payment. Always ask for the unit's hour reading and service history.

What credit score is needed for reefer truck financing?

Most lenders prefer 600+ for competitive rates. 720+ typically qualifies for the most favorable terms. Some specialty lenders work with 550–600 with a larger down payment.

How much down payment is required for refrigerated truck financing?

Typically 10–30%. New reefers may allow 0–15%; high-hour used reefers may require 20–30%. Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with little or no down payment.

Can startups finance reefer trucks?

Yes. Some lenders work with newer cold-chain businesses. Expect 20–30% down, proof of produce or frozen contracts, and strong personal credit. See startup trucking business financing.

New vs. used reefer—which is better to finance?

New reefers offer longer terms, lower rates, and warranty coverage. Used reefers cost less upfront but require close diligence on unit hours, box condition, and refrigerant type. The right choice depends on lane demands and budget.

How quickly can refrigerated truck financing be approved?

Pre-approval: 24–72 hours. Full approval and funding: typically 1–5 business days. Have tax returns, bank statements, equipment details, and reefer unit hours ready.

Can I finance a reefer as an owner-operator?

Yes. Owner-operators can finance reefer trucks and trailers. Lenders typically want produce or frozen contracts, load history, or carrier agreements. Down payments of 15–25% are common. See owner-operator financing.

What documents are needed for reefer truck financing?

Business tax returns (2 years), bank statements (3–6 months), driver's license, and equipment details (VIN, make, model, price). For used equipment, include the reefer unit's make, hour reading, and service records.

Who uses refrigerated trucks?

Produce haulers, frozen food distributors, meat and seafood carriers, dairy, floral, and pharmaceutical transporters all rely on reefers to keep cargo within a controlled temperature range.
