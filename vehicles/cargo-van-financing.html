Definition

A cargo van is a commercial van with an enclosed cargo area, typically built on a van chassis. Common models include Ram Promaster, Ford Transit, and Nissan NV. Cargo vans are used for delivery, trades (plumbing, electrical, HVAC), last-mile logistics, and service businesses. Most cargo vans stay under 26,000 lbs GVWR and do not require a CDL.

Typical Financing Scenarios

Financing terms vary by borrower profile. Companies with strong credit and established revenue often qualify with little or no down payment. Higher-risk scenarios—startups, owner-operators without revenue history, or businesses rebuilding credit—may require 20–30% down, shorter terms, or higher rates.

  • Established businesses: Companies with 2+ years in business often qualify for favorable terms—typically 10–15% down or less.
  • Owner-operators: May qualify with contracts or revenue history. Down payments of 15–25% are common.
  • Startups: Often need 20–30% down, a business plan, and proof of contracts.
  • Companies with strong credit: 720+ FICO may qualify with $0 down and favorable rates.
  • Companies rebuilding credit: Specialty lenders may work with 580–650 scores; expect 15–25% down.

New vs. Used Cargo Van Financing

New cargo vans qualify for 60–84 month terms and 10–15% down. Used cargo van financing typically runs 36–60 months with 20–30% down. Mileage and condition affect valuation. Well-maintained used cargo vans qualify for competitive terms.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Underestimating cargo needs: Choosing a van that is too small for tools or cargo can limit growth and require early replacement.
  • Ignoring fuel economy: High-mileage delivery and service routes make fuel costs significant; compare diesel vs. gasoline options.
  • Skipping pre-purchase inspection: Used vans should be inspected for wear, rust, and mechanical issues before financing.
  • Not verifying GVWR: Confirm the van stays under 26,000 lbs if you want to avoid CDL requirements.

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Licensing requirements for operating a cargo van vary by state, vehicle weight, and business activity. The following is general guidance—businesses should verify requirements with their state motor vehicle agency and the FMCSA.

Driver License Requirements

Most cargo vans stay under 26,000 pounds GVWR and do not require a Commercial Driver's License (CDL). A standard driver's license is typically sufficient. Vehicles over 26,000 lbs GVWR generally require a Class B CDL. Requirements vary by state.

DOT Registration Requirements

Businesses that operate commercial motor vehicles in interstate commerce must register with the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) and obtain a USDOT number. Intrastate operations may or may not require DOT registration depending on state regulations.

Common Questions

Do you need a CDL to drive a cargo van?

Most cargo vans under 26,000 lbs GVWR do not require a CDL. A standard driver's license is typically sufficient.

What cargo vans are commonly financed?

Ram Promaster, Ford Transit, Nissan NV, and similar commercial vans. Compact and full-size variants are both widely financed.

How much does a cargo van cost to finance?

Cargo vans range from $25,000 to $55,000 new depending on make, model, and configuration. Down payments typically run 10–30%.

Can I finance a used cargo van?

Yes. Used cargo van financing is widely available. Terms are typically 36–60 months.

What is the difference between cargo van and box truck?

Cargo vans are smaller, integrated units. Box trucks are larger with separate cab and cargo. Both are commonly financed for delivery.

What credit score is needed to finance a cargo van?

Most lenders prefer 600+ for competitive rates. 720+ typically qualifies for the most favorable terms. Businesses with steady revenue may qualify with lower scores.

How much down payment is required for cargo van financing?

Typically 10–30%. New cargo vans often allow 10–15%; used may require 20–30%. Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with little or no down payment.

Can startups finance cargo vans?

Yes. Some lenders work with newer delivery or service businesses. Expect 20–30% down, proof of contracts or revenue, and strong personal credit.

How long do cargo van loans usually last?

New cargo vans: 60–84 months. Used: 36–60 months depending on age and mileage.

How quickly can cargo van financing be approved?

Pre-approval: 24–72 hours. Full approval and funding: typically 1–5 business days.

What documents are needed for cargo van financing?

Business tax returns (2 years), bank statements (3–6 months), driver's license, and equipment details (make, model, price).

Who uses cargo vans?

Delivery companies, tradespeople (plumbing, electrical, HVAC), last-mile logistics, and service businesses. Cargo vans are versatile for small cargo and tools.
