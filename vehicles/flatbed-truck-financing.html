Financing for Flatbed Trucks & Trailers

Flatbed truck financing covers trucks and trailers with flat, open beds for hauling materials, equipment, and machinery. Used by construction, materials hauling, equipment transport, and logistics. Flatbeds can be straight trucks or tractor-trailer combinations. Both new and used flatbed trucks are widely financed.

Straight Truck vs. Tractor-Trailer

Flatbed trucks can be straight trucks (single unit with flat bed) or tractor-trailer combinations. Semi truck financing covers the tractor; flatbed trailers are often financed separately. Straight truck flatbeds are common for regional hauling. Document chassis, bed length, and trailer specs for accurate valuation.

Typical Financing Scenarios

Financing terms vary by borrower profile. Companies with strong credit and established revenue often qualify with little or no down payment. Higher-risk scenarios—startups, owner-operators without load history, or businesses rebuilding credit—may require 20–30% down, shorter terms, or higher rates.

  • Established trucking companies: Fleets with 2+ years in business typically receive the best terms—often 10–15% down or less.
  • Owner-operators: May qualify with carrier agreements or load history. Down payments of 15–25% are common.
  • Startups: Often need 20–30% down, a business plan, and proof of contracts.
  • Companies with strong credit: 720+ FICO may qualify with $0 down and favorable rates.
  • Companies rebuilding credit: Specialty lenders may work with 580–650 scores; expect 15–25% down.

New vs. Used Flatbed Truck Financing

New flatbed trucks qualify for 60–84 month terms and 10–15% down. Used flatbed truck financing typically runs 36–60 months with 20–30% down. Bed condition and chassis mileage affect valuation. Well-maintained flatbeds retain value.

What Lenders Evaluate

  • Revenue: Haul revenue, contract work, or project revenue.
  • Time in business: 12–24 months minimum; 2+ years for stronger terms.
  • Equipment: Chassis, bed length, trailer specs, and condition.
  • Credit: Personal and business credit.

Common Questions

What credit score is needed to finance a flatbed truck?

Most lenders prefer 600+ for competitive rates. 720+ typically qualifies for the best terms. Construction and materials haulers with contract revenue may qualify with lower scores.

How much down payment is required for flatbed truck financing?

Typically 10–30%. New flatbeds often allow 10–15%; used may require 20–30%. Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with little or no down payment.

Can startups finance flatbed trucks?

Yes. Some lenders work with newer construction or hauling businesses. Expect 20–30% down, proof of contracts, and strong personal credit.

How long do flatbed truck loans usually last?

New flatbed trucks: 60–84 months. Used: 36–60 months depending on age and mileage. Bed condition affects terms.

How quickly can flatbed truck financing be approved?

Pre-approval: 24–72 hours. Full approval and funding: typically 1–5 business days. Have business documentation and equipment details ready.

Can I finance a flatbed trailer?

Yes. Flatbed trailers are commonly financed separately from tractors. Terms are typically 36–60 months for trailers. Tractors and trailers can be financed together or separately.

What documents are needed for flatbed truck financing?

Business tax returns (2 years), bank statements (3–6 months), driver's license, and equipment details (chassis, bed length, trailer specs, price).

How much does a flatbed truck cost to finance?

Flatbed trucks range from $60,000 to $150,000+ depending on chassis and trailer. Down payments typically run 10–30%. Flatbeds have strong resale markets.

What is the difference between flatbed and box truck?

Flatbed trucks have open flat beds for cargo. Box trucks have enclosed cargo areas. Flatbeds suit steel, lumber, machinery, and oversized loads.
