Definition

An agricultural sprayer is equipment for applying herbicides, fungicides, and fertilizers to crops. Self-propelled sprayers are dedicated units with their own chassis. Truck-mounted sprayers are spray systems mounted on a truck. Common brands include John Deere, Case IH, AGCO, and sprayer manufacturers. Used by farmers and custom applicators for precision crop application.

Common Agricultural Sprayer Configurations

  • Truck-mounted sprayer – Spray system on truck chassis; versatile, lower cost
  • Self-propelled sprayer – Dedicated unit; high capacity, precision application
  • Boom sprayer – Wide boom for row crops; common for herbicide and fertilizer
  • Precision agriculture – GPS, variable rate; higher cost, higher efficiency

Typical Financing Scenarios

Financing terms vary by borrower profile. Established applicators and farms with strong credit often qualify with 15–20% down. Higher-risk scenarios—new operations or seasonal revenue—may require 20–30% down.

  • Established applicators: 2+ years in business often qualify for 15–20% down.
  • Farm operations: May qualify with proof of acreage and revenue.
  • New operations: Often need 25–30% down and business plan.

New vs. Used Agricultural Sprayer Financing

New sprayers qualify for 60–84 month terms and 15–20% down. Used sprayer financing typically runs 48–72 months with 20–30% down. Tank condition, pump, and boom affect valuation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Underestimating tank and boom needs: Choosing a sprayer that is too small for acreage can limit productivity and require early replacement.
  • Ignoring chemical compatibility: Tank and pump materials must suit the chemicals applied.
  • Skipping inspection: Used sprayers should be inspected for tank corrosion, pump wear, and boom condition.

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Licensing requirements vary by configuration. Truck-mounted sprayers may require CDL depending on weight. Self-propelled sprayers used on-farm may have farm exemptions. Pesticide applicator certification is typically required for commercial application.

Can You Operate This Equipment Without a CDL?

It depends on configuration and use. Farm use may have exemptions. Road use for custom application may require CDL. Verify state requirements.
