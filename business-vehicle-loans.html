Business Vehicle Loans

Business vehicle loans let you finance the vehicles your company runs on — cargo vans, pickups, box trucks, semi tractors and trailers, or an entire fleet — while preserving cash and building equity in the asset. This guide covers what business vehicle loans are, which vehicles you can finance, loan vs lease, typical rates and terms, down payment and credit requirements, and how to qualify, new or used.

Key Takeaways

  • The vehicle is collateral, so approval is easier than unsecured business financing
  • Terms typically run 36–84 months; rates roughly 7%–18% APR by credit and vehicle
  • Down payment is risk-based — strong-credit businesses can qualify for $0 down
  • Interest and depreciation (including Section 179) may be tax-deductible

Definition

A business vehicle loan (also called commercial vehicle financing or a commercial auto loan) is a secured loan a business uses to purchase a vehicle for commercial use. The lender holds a lien on the vehicle until the loan is repaid. Unlike a personal auto loan, the borrower is the business entity, and the financing is underwritten on the business's revenue, time in business, and credit alongside the owner's personal credit.

Key Facts About Business Vehicle Loans

  • Typical time to decision: 24–72 hours
  • Typical term: 36–84 months
  • Typical rate: 7%–18% APR (credit- and vehicle-dependent)
  • Down payment: 0–25%, risk-based
  • Collateral: the financed vehicle

How Business Vehicle Loans Work

A business vehicle loan follows a straightforward path: you choose the vehicle and price, apply with basic business and owner information, and the lender underwrites the deal using the vehicle as collateral. On approval, the lender pays the seller and you repay in fixed monthly installments over the term. Because the loan is secured by the vehicle, lenders can offer longer terms and more accessible approval than unsecured working-capital products.

  1. Identify the vehicle and purchase price (new or used)
  2. Submit a short application with business and owner details
  3. Provide documentation if requested (bank statements, tax returns for larger amounts)
  4. Review the rate, term, and any down payment
  5. Fund the purchase and put the vehicle into service

Business Vehicle Loan vs Lease

The two main ways to acquire a business vehicle are a loan and a lease. A loan builds equity and ends in ownership; a lease offers lower payments and an easy upgrade path. For vehicles you keep and that hold value — work trucks, semis — a loan is usually the better financial decision.

Rates and Terms

Business vehicle loan rates typically range from 7% to 18% APR for qualified commercial borrowers, with the lowest rates reserved for strong credit, newer vehicles, and shorter terms. Terms commonly run 36–84 months — longer for new heavy vehicles with strong collateral, shorter for older or higher-mileage used units. Manufacturer captive programs sometimes offer promotional rates on new dealer purchases.

What Lenders Evaluate

  • Time in business: Most prefer 12–24 months; the strongest programs want 2+ years. Startups can still qualify, often with more down.
  • Revenue and cash flow: Enough to comfortably support the payment.
  • Credit: Personal and business credit shape rate and approval; 650+ typically earns competitive terms.
  • Vehicle: Age, mileage, condition, and resale value. Newer vehicles from dealers get the best terms.

New vs Used Business Vehicle Financing

New vehicles qualify for longer terms, lower rates, and higher advance amounts, plus possible manufacturer promotions. Used-vehicle financing is more flexible than many borrowers expect — especially for trucks and trailers that hold value — but expect shorter terms, slightly higher rates, and lender limits on age and mileage. Match the loan term to how long you'll keep and use the vehicle.

Tax Treatment

With a business vehicle loan, the interest portion of each payment is generally deductible, and you can depreciate the vehicle — frequently claiming a substantial first-year deduction under Section 179 (subject to per-vehicle limits for passenger autos and different rules for heavier vehicles). A lease, by contrast, is typically deducted as a payment expense. The right structure for your taxes depends on the vehicle and how it's used — confirm with your accountant.

Common Questions

What is a business vehicle loan?

A business vehicle loan is commercial financing used to purchase a vehicle for business use — cargo vans, pickups, box trucks, semi tractors, or a fleet. The vehicle serves as collateral, the business (not the individual) is the borrower, and the loan is repaid in fixed monthly payments, typically over 36–84 months. Because the vehicle secures the loan, these loans are often easier to qualify for than unsecured business loans.

What credit score do I need for a business vehicle loan?

Many lenders prefer 600+ for competitive rates, and 680+ qualifies for the best terms. Specialty lenders finance business vehicles for borrowers in the 550–600 range with a larger down payment. Because the vehicle is collateral, approval is generally more accessible than for unsecured financing.

How much down payment is required for a business vehicle loan?

Typically 0–20%, risk-based. Established businesses with strong credit often qualify with $0 down. Startups or borrowers rebuilding credit may need 10–25%. New vehicles usually require less down than older, high-mileage used units.

What types of vehicles can a business finance?

Nearly any vehicle used commercially: cargo and sprinter vans, pickups, box/straight trucks, semi tractors and trailers, dump trucks, service and utility trucks, and full fleets. Both new and used are widely financed.

Is it better to lease or take a loan on a business vehicle?

A loan is usually better for vehicles you'll keep long-term and that hold value (semis, work trucks) — you build equity and own the asset. Leasing can make sense for vehicles you cycle frequently or want off the balance sheet. A loan lets you claim depreciation and Section 179; a true lease lets you expense the payments.

Are business vehicle loan payments tax deductible?

The interest portion is generally deductible, and you can depreciate the vehicle — often a large first-year deduction under Section 179 (subject to limits for passenger vehicles). The principal portion is not deductible. Confirm your specific situation with your accountant.
