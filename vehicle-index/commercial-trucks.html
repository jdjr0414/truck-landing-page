Commercial Trucks

Commercial trucks used for freight, hauling, logistics, and materials. Each page covers financing requirements, typical costs, and terms for that vehicle type.

Key Takeaways

  • Commercial vehicle financing covers a wide range of truck types and equipment
  • Typical financing terms range from 36-84 months depending on equipment
  • Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with little or no down payment
  • Approval typically takes 24-72 hours for qualified applicants

Commercial Truck Types and Use Cases

Semi trucks (day cabs and sleepers) haul freight over regional and long-haul routes. Day cabs suit regional routes; sleepers support overnight operations. Cost: $120k�$200k new. Typical financing: 60�84 months. See Semi Truck Financing and Day Cab Semi Financing.

Dump trucks haul construction materials, aggregates, and asphalt. Used by construction, hauling, and municipal contractors. Cost: $80k�$180k new. Financing terms: 48�72 months. See Dump Truck Financing.

Box trucks are used for cargo delivery, moving, and retail distribution. Lower cost ($35k�$80k) and often shorter terms. See Box Truck Financing.

Flatbed, tanker, and refrigerated trucks serve specialized freight. Flatbeds haul steel, lumber, and machinery. Tankers and reefers require additional endorsements. See Flatbed Truck Financing, Tanker Truck Financing, and Refrigerated Truck Financing.

Tow trucks, cement trucks, and service trucks round out commercial truck categories. Each has dedicated financing pages with requirements and typical terms.

Financing Requirements by Truck Type

Most commercial trucks require a CDL for operation. See Commercial Truck License Requirements and CDL Class A vs Class B. Semi trucks, flatbeds, tankers, and refrigerated trucks typically require Class A. Dump trucks, box trucks, and cement trucks often require Class B. Box trucks under 26,000 lbs GVWR may not require a CDL. Lenders do not issue the license but may verify you have the required credentials for the equipment.

Typical down payment: 10�30% depending on credit and equipment. New equipment often allows 10�15%; used may require 20�30%. Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with $0 down. See Typical Down Payments.

Typical terms: New: 60�84 months. Used: 24�60 months depending on age and mileage. Semi trucks have the strongest collateral market and often qualify for the longest terms.

What Lenders Evaluate

Lenders consider credit score, business revenue, time in business, and equipment type. Semi trucks and tankers often receive favorable terms due to strong resale value. Box trucks and lighter equipment may have shorter terms. DOT and MC authority may be verified for freight haulers. See DOT Registration Requirements and DOT Number for Trucking Business.

Documentation Requirements

Commercial truck financing typically requires: business tax returns (2 years), bank statements (3�6 months), driver's license, and equipment specs or invoice. For freight haulers: DOT number, MC authority, and insurance may be requested. See What Documents Needed for Truck Financing.

Common Questions

What commercial trucks can I finance?

Semi trucks, dump trucks, box trucks, flatbeds, tankers, refrigerated trucks, tow trucks, cement trucks, logging trucks, and service trucks. Each has a dedicated financing page.

How much do commercial trucks cost?

Semi: $120K�$200K new. Dump: $80K�$180K. Box: $35K�$80K. Flatbed: $80K�$150K. Tanker: similar to semi. See equipment cost pages for details.

What industries use commercial trucks?

Freight, logistics, construction, hauling, towing, delivery, oilfield, materials. See industry pages for sector-specific financing.

How much down payment for a semi truck?

Typically 10�30%. New semis often allow 10�15%; used may require 20�30%. Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with $0 down. See How Much Down for Semi Truck.

Can startups finance commercial trucks?

Yes. Some lenders work with newer businesses. Expect 20�30% down and proof of revenue or contracts. See Can Startup Trucking Companies Get Financing.

Choosing the Right Commercial Truck

Match the truck to your business model. Freight haulers need semi trucks or flatbeds. Construction and materials haulers need dump trucks. Local delivery and moving companies often use box trucks. Tow operators need tow trucks or rollbacks. Each type has different operating costs, CDL requirements, and financing terms. See Dump Truck vs Semi Truck for a comparison of two common choices.

Approval Timeline

Simple applications: 24�72 hours. Complex deals (startups, challenged credit, large amounts): 1�2 weeks. Pre-approval speeds the process once equipment is selected. See How Fast Can You Get Truck Financing.
