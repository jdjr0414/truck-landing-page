How Much Does a Mining Truck Cost?

A small off-highway haul truck (40-ton payload) costs $800,000–$1,500,000 new. A mid-size mining truck (100-ton class) runs $3,500,000–$5,000,000. Large mining haul trucks (250-ton class) cost $5,000,000–$8,000,000. The largest trucks in the world (400-ton class) cost $8,000,000–$10,000,000+. No CDL required — mining trucks are off-highway vehicles only.

Key Takeaways

  • Small haul truck (40-ton class): $800,000 – $1,500,000
  • Mid-size (100-ton class, Cat 777): $3,500,000 – $5,000,000
  • Large (250-ton class, Cat 793): $5,000,000 – $8,000,000
  • No CDL required (off-highway only)

Mining Truck Tires — A Major Cost Factor

Mining truck tires are one of the largest ongoing operating costs. A single tire for a 100-ton class truck (63-inch) costs $30,000–$50,000. A full set of six tires costs $180,000–$300,000. Tires last 6,000–12,000 hours depending on haul road conditions, speed, and load. Tire cost per hour can exceed fuel cost in adverse conditions, making haul road quality a critical cost management factor.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a mining truck different from a construction dump truck?

A construction dump truck (Class 7–8 highway dump truck) is a licensed highway vehicle used on public roads to haul construction materials — it weighs 20,000–80,000 lbs loaded and costs $80,000–$200,000. A mining truck (off-highway haul truck) is a purpose-built off-highway vehicle that never goes on public roads — it weighs 100,000–1,000,000+ lbs loaded and costs $800,000–$10,000,000. They serve completely different applications and are not interchangeable. See our dump truck cost guide for highway dump truck pricing.

Can you buy a used mining truck?

Yes. Used mining trucks are actively traded, particularly as mines upgrade fleets or close operations. Major auction platforms (Ritchie Bros., IronPlanet), OEM dealer used equipment programs, and direct mine-to-buyer transactions are the common acquisition channels. Condition assessment for used mining trucks includes engine hours, tire condition, frame inspection, and component rebuild history. A well-maintained used truck with documented service history at 40–50% of the hours to major rebuild can be a strong value proposition.
