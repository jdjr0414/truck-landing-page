How Much Does a Box Truck Cost?

Box truck costs vary by size class, body length, and whether the unit has a liftgate. New box trucks range from $35,000 for a 16-ft unit to $90,000+ for a new 26-ft truck. Used box trucks range from $15,000 to $55,000. This guide breaks down pricing by class and financing options.

Key Takeaways

  • New 26-ft box trucks typically cost $65,000–$90,000
  • Used box trucks under 150,000 miles typically run $25,000–$50,000
  • Most box trucks under 26,001 lbs GVWR do not require a CDL
  • Liftgate adds $3,000–$8,000 to purchase price

What Affects Box Truck Pricing

Body length is the primary price driver — a 26-ft box truck costs $30,000–$40,000 more than a 16-ft unit. The 26-ft class is the largest non-CDL size, which explains its popularity and premium pricing.

Liftgate adds $3,000–$8,000 to the purchase price. Hydraulic liftgates are standard for delivery operations; tuck-under liftgates are cleaner for dock work. Most delivery and moving businesses need a liftgate.

Chassis brand affects price and reliability. Isuzu, Ford (E-Series and F-650), Freightliner, and International are the most common platforms. Isuzu NPR/NRR models are popular in the 16–22 ft range; Freightliner M2 is common in the larger classes.

Mileage and age are the key used truck variables. A 3-year-old 26-ft box truck with 80,000 miles is significantly more valuable than the same truck with 200,000 miles. Box trucks operated in urban last-mile delivery accumulate mileage quickly — 50,000–80,000 miles per year is common for daily delivery operations.

CDL Requirements for Box Trucks

Box trucks at or below 26,000 lbs GVWR do not require a CDL under federal regulations. The 26-ft box truck is specifically designed to stay at or near the 26,000 lb threshold — it is the largest truck most owner-operators can drive without a CDL. This is a major reason for its popularity in last-mile delivery, moving, and owner-operated delivery businesses.

Trucks over 26,001 lbs GVWR require a Class B CDL at minimum. Some 26-ft trucks with heavy liftgates, refrigeration units, or full loads may exceed the 26,000 lb threshold even if the chassis GVWR is listed at 26,000 lbs — operators should verify their loaded weight.

Common Box Truck Uses

Last-mile delivery: Amazon DSP, FedEx Ground, UPS contractor routes. The 26-ft box truck is a standard vehicle for independent delivery service partners.

Moving and storage: residential and commercial moves. Box trucks are the standard moving vehicle for owner-operated moving companies.

Food and beverage distribution: bakery, produce, and dry goods delivery. Often paired with refrigeration units for temperature-sensitive products.

Medical equipment and supplies: hospital and clinic supply delivery. Box trucks provide the enclosed, secure cargo space required.

Retail distribution: store-to-store merchandise delivery for regional retail chains.

How to Finance a Box Truck

Box truck financing is widely available from equipment lenders, commercial banks, and specialty truck financing companies. The truck itself typically serves as collateral. Most lenders require:

  • At least 6 months in business (startup financing available with higher down payments)
  • Business bank statements showing consistent revenue
  • Credit score of 580+ for most standard programs
  • Commercial driver's license if the truck GVWR exceeds 26,000 lbs

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a new 26-ft box truck cost?

A new 26-ft box truck typically costs $65,000–$90,000. The price varies by chassis (Freightliner, International, Hino), liftgate type, and any additional upfitting such as refrigeration or shelving.

What is the most common box truck size for delivery businesses?

The 26-ft box truck is the most popular commercial size because it offers maximum cargo volume at the largest non-CDL GVWR threshold. The 16-ft and 20-ft classes are popular for urban delivery where maneuverability matters more than cargo capacity.

Does a box truck require commercial insurance?

Yes. Box trucks operated commercially require commercial auto insurance rather than personal auto insurance. For delivery or for-hire transport, additional cargo insurance and liability coverage may be required by clients or contracts.
