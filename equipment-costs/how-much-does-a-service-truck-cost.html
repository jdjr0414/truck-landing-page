How Much Does a Service Truck Cost?

A service truck with a standard aluminum utility body costs $60,000–$120,000 new. Specialized configurations — lube trucks, mechanics trucks with cranes, heavy-duty utility service units — run $100,000–$200,000+. Used service trucks range from $25,000 to $90,000. Upfitting adds significant cost on top of the chassis.

Key Takeaways

  • Base chassis (Class 4–5): $45,000–$80,000 new
  • Utility body upfitting: $15,000–$40,000 on top of chassis
  • Most service trucks under 26,001 lbs do not require a CDL
  • Upfitted lube trucks and mechanics trucks: $120,000–$200,000+

Common Uses for Service Trucks

HVAC and plumbing service: the most common service truck application. A utility body truck with compartments for tools, parts, and refrigerant cylinders. The Class 4–5 chassis with aluminum utility body is the standard configuration.

Electrical service: similar to HVAC; a utility body truck with conduit racks and wire storage. Some electrical service trucks include a small generator or welder upfit.

Fleet and equipment maintenance: mechanics trucks with a service crane allow on-site repair of construction equipment and heavy trucks. These are more expensive due to the crane and heavy-duty upfitting.

Lube and fluid service: oil change and fluid service for construction equipment, farm machinery, and fleet vehicles. Lube trucks carry multiple oil and fluid tanks, pumps, and meters.

Utility and telecom: utility companies and telecom contractors use service trucks for infrastructure maintenance. Often paired with bucket truck operations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I finance a service truck upfit separately from the chassis?

Yes. Many equipment lenders can finance the chassis and upfit as a single package. Alternatively, the chassis is financed through a truck lender and the upfit through an equipment or commercial lender. Financing the complete truck-and-upfit package in one loan is typically simpler.

What is the most popular service truck for HVAC contractors?

The Isuzu NPR-HD and Ford F-450/F-550 with aluminum utility bodies are the most common HVAC service truck configurations. The Class 4–5 range offers the right payload capacity for tools, parts, and refrigerant without requiring a CDL. For more, see service truck financing.
