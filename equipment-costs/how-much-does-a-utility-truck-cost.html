How Much Does a Utility Truck Cost?

A new digger derrick (pole-setting truck) costs $150,000–$350,000. A line truck with telescoping aerial device costs $180,000–$400,000. Basic utility service trucks (Class 5–6) run $80,000–$130,000. Used digger derricks and line trucks range from $70,000 to $200,000+ depending on age and condition.

Key Takeaways

  • Digger derrick (new): $150,000 – $350,000
  • Line truck with aerial device (new): $180,000 – $400,000
  • Most utility trucks require a Class B CDL
  • Used utility trucks: $70,000 – $200,000

Digger Derrick vs. Line Truck

Digger derrick: The primary vehicle for utility pole installation and replacement. Combines a hydraulic earth auger (digger) for drilling the pole hole with a derrick boom and winch for lifting and placing poles. Also used for equipment installation on existing poles. Digger derricks are the backbone of electric distribution construction crews.

Line truck: A truck with a telescoping or articulating aerial device (sometimes called a bucket truck or aerial device truck) that lifts line workers to the height of distribution or transmission lines. Line trucks for utility use are often equipped with insulated booms and platforms for energized line work. Different from standard bucket trucks used by tree services or telecom — utility-grade line trucks meet higher insulation and load ratings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a utility truck and a bucket truck?

The terms overlap but are used in different contexts. In the electric utility industry, a "line truck" or "utility truck" refers specifically to a Class 7–8 vehicle with a high-capacity insulated aerial device rated for energized line work. A "bucket truck" is a broader term used for any truck with an aerial work platform — including the lighter-duty units used by tree services, sign companies, and telecom contractors. Utility-grade line trucks have significantly higher insulation ratings, boom capacities, and reliability standards than commercial bucket trucks.

Can a line contractor use the same trucks as a utility company?

Yes. Line construction contractors who build and maintain electric distribution and transmission systems use the same digger derricks and line trucks as the utilities themselves. Many utilities outsource construction work to independent line contractors, who must own or lease the same equipment. For utility contracting, see utility truck financing.
