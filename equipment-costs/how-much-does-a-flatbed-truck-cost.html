How Much Does a Flatbed Truck Cost?

A new Class 4–5 flatbed truck (Ford F-550, Ram 5500, Isuzu NRR) with flatbed body costs $55,000–$90,000. A new Class 6 medium-duty flatbed costs $80,000–$120,000. Used flatbed trucks range from $20,000 to $75,000. This page covers flatbed TRUCKS — single-unit vehicles — not flatbed semi trailers.

Key Takeaways

  • New Class 4–5 flatbed truck: $55,000–$90,000
  • New Class 6 medium-duty flatbed: $80,000–$120,000
  • Most Class 4–5 flatbeds do not require a CDL
  • Flatbed truck vs. flatbed trailer: these are different vehicles

Common Uses for Flatbed Trucks

Construction material delivery: lumber, steel, pipe, and building materials that are too long or awkward for an enclosed box truck. A 12-ft flatbed on a Class 5 chassis handles most jobsite material deliveries.

Landscaping: sod, stone, mulch, pavers, and equipment transport. The flatbed's open design loads and unloads faster than a box truck for bulk landscape materials.

Equipment transport: transporting small construction equipment — plate compactors, skid steers up to a certain size, trailers. Class 5–6 flatbeds can haul a surprisingly wide range of compact equipment when combined with proper ramps.

Nursery and farm supply: potted trees, pallets, bulk aggregate, and farm equipment. A flatbed is the workhorse vehicle for rural supply businesses.

Towing and recovery (light): small and medium flatbed trucks are used as tow trucks for light vehicles. See how much does a tow truck cost for tow-specific pricing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the payload capacity of a Class 5 flatbed truck?

A Class 5 flatbed truck (19,501–26,000 lbs GVWR) with a tare weight of approximately 8,000–10,000 lbs typically has a payload capacity of 10,000–16,000 lbs. Actual payload depends on the specific chassis, cab, and body configuration. Always verify the specific truck's GVWR and tare weight.

Should I buy a flatbed truck or a pickup with a gooseneck trailer?

A flatbed truck is better for frequent in-town and jobsite deliveries where you want a single maneuverable unit. A pickup with a gooseneck trailer offers more total capacity (especially for equipment) and the flexibility to drop the trailer when not needed. Hotshot operators typically use the pickup-trailer combination. Local contractors and landscapers often prefer a single flatbed unit. See hotshot truck cost for the pickup-trailer option.
