How Much Does a Refrigerated Truck Cost?

A new Class 4–5 refrigerated straight truck costs $80,000–$130,000 with refrigeration unit. Class 6 reefer trucks run $100,000–$160,000. Used refrigerated trucks range from $35,000 for older units to $90,000+ for late-model trucks in good condition. The refrigeration unit (Thermo King or Carrier) accounts for $15,000–$30,000 of the total cost and is the highest-maintenance component.

Key Takeaways

  • New Class 4–5 reefer truck (with refrigeration unit): $80,000 – $130,000
  • Refrigeration unit alone (Thermo King / Carrier): $15,000 – $30,000
  • Class 4–5 units do not require a CDL; Class 6 typically does
  • Used refrigerated trucks: $35,000 – $90,000

Refrigerated Truck vs. Refrigerated Trailer: What's the Difference?

This guide covers straight refrigerated trucks — single-unit vehicles where the refrigerated box and chassis are one integrated unit. No tractor required. These are the standard vehicle for local and regional cold chain delivery.

Refrigerated trailers (also called reefer trailers) are separate 48- or 53-foot trailers pulled by a semi-truck tractor. They are used for long-haul and high-volume distribution. For refrigerated trailer pricing, see our refrigerated trailer cost guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most important thing to check on a used refrigerated truck?

The refrigeration unit. Thermo King and Carrier units accumulate significant wear and have expensive repair costs — compressor replacement alone can run $8,000–$15,000. When evaluating a used reefer truck, pull the refrigeration unit's service history, check for refrigerant leaks, test the temperature pull-down speed (how fast it reaches set temperature from ambient), and inspect the box insulation for moisture damage or delamination.

Can the refrigeration unit run while the engine is off?

Most straight truck refrigeration units have their own diesel engine and run independently of the chassis engine. This allows the truck to maintain temperature at delivery stops without idling the main engine. Some units also have an electric standby option for plugging in at a dock or warehouse.
