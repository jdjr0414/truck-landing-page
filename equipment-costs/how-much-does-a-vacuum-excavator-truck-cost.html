How Much Does a Vacuum Excavator Truck Cost?

A new full-size vacuum excavator truck (hydrovac) costs $250,000–$500,000 depending on tank capacity, boom reach, and water system specifications. Smaller truck-mounted units start at $150,000–$250,000. Trailer-mounted vacuum excavators start at $80,000–$150,000. Used hydrovac trucks range from $100,000 to $300,000+ for well-maintained units.

Key Takeaways

  • New full-size hydrovac truck: $250,000 – $500,000
  • New trailer-mounted unit: $80,000 – $150,000
  • Used hydrovac trucks: $100,000 – $300,000
  • Billing rates: $200 – $450 per hour; $1,600 – $3,600 per day

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a hot water hydrovac worth the premium cost?

In cold climates where ground freezes (Canada, northern U.S.), hot water hydrovac capability is often essential — cold water cannot break up frozen ground. Hot water units carry a $30,000–$70,000 premium over cold water equivalents, but in northern markets, a cold water unit may be unusable 3–5 months per year. In southern U.S. markets, cold water units are sufficient year-round.

What is potholing and why is it done with hydrovac?

Potholing (also called daylighting) is the process of exposing buried utilities before excavation begins — to visually confirm their exact location, depth, and condition. Mechanical excavation near buried utilities risks striking and damaging gas lines, fiber optic cables, or power conduits. Hydrovac is the preferred method for potholing because it can expose utilities without mechanical contact, reducing the risk of costly utility strikes and the associated liability.
