How Much Does a Shuttle Bus Cost?

A new 24-passenger cutaway shuttle bus costs $55,000–$90,000. Sprinter-based 14-passenger vans run $45,000–$75,000. Larger 35-passenger medium-duty shuttle buses cost $80,000–$120,000. Used shuttle buses range from $15,000 for older high-mileage units to $60,000 for late-model low-mileage airport or hotel shuttles.

Key Takeaways

  • New 24-passenger cutaway shuttle: $55,000 – $90,000
  • New Sprinter-based 14-passenger van: $45,000 – $75,000
  • Used shuttle buses: $15,000 – $60,000

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular shuttle bus chassis?

The Ford E-450 Super Duty cutaway is the most common chassis for hotel, airport, and commercial shuttle buses in the U.S., followed by the Ram ProMaster, Chevrolet Express, and Isuzu NPR. The Ford E-450 has dominated the cutaway passenger bus market for decades due to wide dealer network availability and body builder compatibility. For Sprinter-based shuttles, the Mercedes-Benz Sprinter 2500 and 3500 are standard.

How is a shuttle bus different from a charter bus?

Shuttle buses are smaller (12–35 passengers), used for short-route repeat-service operations, and cost $45,000–$120,000. Charter buses (motorcoaches) are full-size (45–57 passengers), used for point-to-point and tour operations, and cost $400,000–$700,000+.
