How Much Does a Roll-Off Truck Cost?

A new roll-off truck costs $120,000–$200,000. Used roll-off trucks range from $50,000 to $130,000. Containers add $3,000–$6,000 each. A complete roll-off dumpster business startup — truck plus 10–15 containers — typically requires $150,000–$275,000 in equipment. This guide covers roll-off and hooklift truck pricing, container costs, CDL requirements, and financing.

Key Takeaways

  • New roll-off truck: $120,000–$200,000
  • Roll-off containers: $3,000–$6,000 each new
  • 10-container starter fleet: $30,000–$60,000 additional
  • Class B CDL required for most roll-off operations

Roll-Off vs. Hooklift: Which System?

Roll-off trucks use a cable hoist that pulls containers onto a tilting frame. Roll-off is the standard system for large construction dumpsters. Containers are cheaper and simpler. Most established dumpster rental companies use roll-off.

Hooklift trucks use a hook-and-lift system that can handle a wider variety of container types — open-top dumpsters, flatbed bodies, debris boxes, liquid containers. Hooklifts are more versatile but containers must be equipped with the hook-compatible attachment. Hooklift is gaining popularity with contractors who want to handle multiple container types with one truck.

CDL Requirements

Roll-off trucks are heavy commercial vehicles that typically exceed 26,001 lbs GVWR when loaded. A Class B CDL is required for single-unit roll-off trucks. Drivers should also be aware of axle weight limits and local permit requirements for container transport on public roads.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many containers do I need to start a roll-off business?

Most operators recommend a minimum of 8–12 containers to start — enough to keep a truck productively deployed without too many containers sitting empty. As the business scales, the rule of thumb is 8–12 containers per truck. For a 3-truck operation, 25–40 containers is typical.

Can I finance both the truck and containers together?

Yes. Many equipment lenders can finance the truck and a container fleet as a package. The truck typically serves as primary collateral. Containers can be financed separately as a fleet or bundled with the truck.
