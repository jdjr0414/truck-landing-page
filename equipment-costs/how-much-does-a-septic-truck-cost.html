How Much Does a Septic Truck Cost?

A new septic pumper truck costs $120,000–$250,000 for a Class 6–7 unit with a 2,000–3,500 gallon tank. Larger 4,000–5,000 gallon Class 8 tankers run $200,000–$350,000. Combination jetter-vac trucks cost $150,000–$350,000. Used septic trucks range from $45,000 for older units to $150,000+ for well-maintained late-model trucks.

Key Takeaways

  • New Class 6–7 septic pumper truck: $120,000 – $250,000
  • New combination jetter-vac: $150,000 – $350,000
  • Used septic trucks: $45,000 – $150,000
  • Class B CDL required for most units when loaded

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a septic truck and a vac truck?

A septic truck (also called a septic pumper) is specifically designed for pumping residential and commercial septic tanks and grease traps. Vac trucks are a broader category that includes septic trucks, industrial vacuum trucks, and liquid waste haulers. Septic trucks are optimized for the specific viscosity and odor requirements of septage — sealed tanks, heavy-duty pumps, and specialized hoses. Most septic trucks are also used for portable restroom service, grease trap pumping, and similar applications. See our vac truck cost guide for industrial vacuum truck pricing.

Is a combination jetter-vac worth the premium cost?

A combination jetter-vac (also called a hydro-jetter-vac or sewer jetter-vac) can both pump septic tanks and use pressurized water to jet and clear drain lines and sewer pipes. The premium over a standard pumper runs $30,000–$100,000, but it significantly expands the services the truck can perform — adding drain cleaning, sewer line maintenance, and grease trap jetting. For operators who want to offer both pumping and jetting services, a combination unit provides better revenue per truck. For more, see septic truck financing.
