How Much Does a Snow Plow Truck Cost?

A pickup-based snow plow truck costs $45,000–$75,000 new with plow attachment. Medium-duty commercial plow trucks run $80,000–$140,000. Municipal heavy-duty plow units with spreaders can reach $200,000–$400,000+. Used pickup plow trucks range from $20,000 to $55,000 depending on age, mileage, and plow condition.

Key Takeaways

  • Pickup plow truck (Class 2–4 + plow): $45,000–$75,000 new
  • Plow attachment alone: $4,000–$15,000 installed depending on type and size
  • Most commercial plow trucks do not require a CDL
  • Used pickup plow trucks: $20,000–$55,000

Revenue Potential vs. Equipment Cost

Snow plowing is a seasonal business. Understanding revenue potential relative to equipment cost is critical when evaluating whether to buy or finance a plow truck.

Residential route revenue: a single pickup plow truck running a residential snow removal route can generate $300–$600 per inch of snow per route, with routes of 20–40 residential accounts. A season with 10 plow events generates $30,000–$60,000+ from a single residential route in markets with consistent snowfall.

Commercial lot contracts: seasonal commercial contracts — parking lots, retail centers, HOAs — typically run $5,000–$20,000 per season per account for medium-duty operations. A truck running 5–8 commercial accounts can generate $40,000–$80,000+ per season.

Seasonality risk: unlike other commercial vehicles, plow trucks are only productive during snow events. Warm winters with low snowfall directly reduce revenue. Many operators offset this by using their trucks for landscaping, hauling, or other services in the off-season.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I buy new or used for a plow truck?

Used pickup plow trucks offer the lowest entry cost but carry more risk — plow trucks accumulate significant wear on the front suspension, frame, and transmission from repeated plow impacts. Buying used requires careful inspection of plow mount points, front end components, and transmission condition. New trucks with plow prep packages have factory warranties that cover these high-wear areas. For operators starting a new route, a certified pre-owned pickup with a newer plow attachment is often the best balance of cost and reliability.

What is the best brand of plow for a commercial operation?

Boss, Western, Fisher, and Meyer are the most common commercial plow brands in the U.S. Boss and Western are widely considered the most durable for heavy commercial use. Fisher is dominant in the Northeast market. For V-plows and expandable plows, Boss and Western both have strong dealer networks for parts and service. For more, see snow plow truck financing.
