How Much Does a Street Sweeper Cost?

A new mechanical broom street sweeper costs $100,000–$200,000. Regenerative air sweepers run $150,000–$350,000. Vacuum sweepers for heavy or industrial applications range from $200,000–$500,000+. Used sweepers start at $60,000 for older mechanical broom units and range to $250,000+ for late-model regenerative air units.

The Three Main Sweeper Technologies

Mechanical broom sweepers use rotating side brooms (gutter brooms) to sweep debris from curbs and edges toward the center of the sweep path, and a main cylindrical broom to pick up the debris and convey it into a hopper. They are effective at picking up heavier debris — gravel, leaves, larger particles — but generate dust and are increasingly restricted in air quality non-attainment areas. Cost-effective and durable, mechanical broom sweepers are the most common entry point for private sweeping contractors.

Regenerative air sweepers blast a high-volume air stream at the pavement surface to loosen fine debris, then vacuum the debris and air back into the system — filtering the air and dumping the debris into a sealed hopper. They produce significantly less dust than mechanical broom units and meet PM10 and PM2.5 standards required in many California Air Resources Board (CARB) and EPA non-attainment areas. Many municipal and highway contracts now specify regenerative air. Higher purchase cost, but often required for contract compliance.

Vacuum sweepers use pure suction to collect debris without mechanical brooms. They are most effective for fine material, standing water, and industrial applications — but less effective on heavy debris. Used for airport runways, industrial facilities, and applications requiring sealed containment of hazardous material.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I buy mechanical broom or regenerative air?

It depends on your contract requirements and market. If you're bidding municipal or highway contracts, check whether the specifications require regenerative air or PM10 compliance — many do, particularly in California, Texas, and other states with air quality non-attainment designations. For private parking lot sweeping without regulatory requirements, a mechanical broom sweeper offers a lower entry cost and handles heavier debris effectively.

What is a high-dump sweeper?

A high-dump sweeper can elevate its hopper to dump debris directly into a dump truck or container — typically 8–12 feet high — without requiring a separate transfer point. This is useful for high-volume municipal operations where the sweeper is continuously working and needs to offload debris quickly.
