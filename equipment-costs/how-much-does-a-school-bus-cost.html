How Much Does a School Bus Cost?

A new full-size Type C school bus costs $90,000–$120,000. New Type D transit-style buses run $100,000–$130,000. New Type A mini school buses cost $40,000–$80,000. Electric school buses run $250,000–$400,000+ but are covered by federal grants for qualifying districts. Used school buses range from $5,000 for older retired units to $65,000 for mid-life buses in good condition.

Key Takeaways

  • New Type C school bus: $90,000 – $120,000
  • New Type D transit school bus: $100,000 – $130,000
  • Used school buses: $5,000 – $65,000 (significant supply from fleet retirements)
  • Class B CDL with P and S endorsements required

Frequently Asked Questions

Are school buses street legal for non-school use?

Yes. Retired school buses can be registered and operated for non-school transportation, including church buses, camp buses, and private group transportation. Operators should check state requirements — some states require school bus color (National School Bus Yellow) to be changed if the bus is no longer used for student transportation. Standard vehicle registration and appropriate CDL endorsements are required for all commercial passenger operations.

What is a "skoolie" and how does it affect pricing?

A "skoolie" is a retired school bus converted into a recreational vehicle or mobile home. The conversion market has increased demand for retired full-size school buses in recent years, modestly raising prices for older units in good structural condition. Type C and D buses in reasonable condition now often sell for $10,000–$25,000 even at 15–20 years old, partly due to conversion interest. Buyers purchasing for transportation use should prioritize mechanical condition over cosmetics.
