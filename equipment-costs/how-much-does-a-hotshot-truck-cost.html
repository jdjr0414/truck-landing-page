How Much Does a Hotshot Truck Cost?

A hotshot truck is typically a heavy-duty pickup paired with a gooseneck trailer. New 1-ton dually trucks run $55,000–$90,000. A trailer adds $15,000–$40,000. Total new hotshot setup: $70,000–$130,000. Used setups can be assembled for $35,000–$90,000. This guide covers truck and trailer costs, CDL requirements, and financing options.

Key Takeaways

  • New hotshot truck (1-ton dually): $55,000–$90,000
  • Gooseneck trailer (40-ft flatbed): $15,000–$35,000 new
  • Total new hotshot setup: $70,000–$130,000
  • CDL may be required if GCWR exceeds 26,001 lbs

CDL Requirements for Hotshot

Whether a hotshot operator needs a CDL depends on the combined gross combination weight rating (GCWR) of the truck and trailer, not the weight of the truck alone.

If the GCWR exceeds 26,001 lbs, a Class A CDL is required. Many hotshot setups — particularly with a loaded Class 4 truck and a 40-ft trailer hauling heavy freight — exceed this threshold. Operators should calculate their specific loaded GCWR.

Some hotshot operators stay under the 26,001 lb threshold intentionally by using lighter Class 3 trucks and managing load weight — allowing operation without a CDL. This limits payload capacity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Ford F-450 or Ram 4500 better for hotshot?

Both are capable hotshot trucks. The F-450 has a large dealer and parts network. The Ram 4500 (commercial-grade Cummins diesel) is preferred by some operators for powertrain longevity on high-mileage routes. The choice often comes down to brand loyalty, regional dealer availability, and resale market in your area.

Can I start hotshot trucking with a used truck?

Yes. Many hotshot operators start with a used 1-ton dually in the $30,000–$45,000 range and a used gooseneck trailer in the $10,000–$18,000 range, keeping total startup equipment cost under $65,000. Used equipment carries higher maintenance risk but significantly reduces debt service. See hotshot trucking equipment list for the full checklist.
