How Much Does a Logging Truck Cost?

A new logging truck (tractor) costs $150,000–$250,000+. A log trailer adds $25,000–$60,000. Truck-mounted knuckleboom loaders add $50,000–$120,000. Total new logging truck setup: $175,000–$400,000. Used logging trucks range from $60,000 to $150,000 depending on age, mileage, and configuration.

Why Logging Trucks Cost More Than Standard Semi Trucks

Logging trucks are configured for severe duty cycle that exceeds typical over-the-road semi truck operations:

Heavy payload on rough terrain: log trucks routinely operate on logging roads — unpaved, steep, rutted surfaces that accelerate frame, suspension, and drivetrain wear. The chassis must be heavier-duty than a standard OTR tractor.

High torque demand: moving fully loaded log trucks out of the woods requires high torque at low RPM. Most logging trucks use high-output diesel engines — Cummins X15, Paccar MX-13, Detroit DD15 — with ratings of 500–600 HP and 1,650–2,050 lb-ft of torque.

Log bunk and bolster configuration: the tractor must be configured with log bunks and bolsters to secure the logs. This requires specialized equipment mounting that standard tractors do not have.

Knuckleboom loaders: self-loading trucks — the dominant configuration in many timber markets — require a truck-mounted hydraulic loader that adds $50,000–$120,000 and significant weight to the tractor.

CDL and Permit Requirements

Commercial log trucks operating tractor-trailer combinations over 26,001 lbs GVWR require a Class A CDL. Most states have specific oversize/overweight permit programs for loaded log trucks, which regularly exceed standard highway weight limits. Annual or trip permits are required in most logging states.

Some states have specific logging industry exemptions that allow intrastate log hauling on private or forest roads without a CDL — these vary significantly by state and should be confirmed with the state DOT before operating.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common logging truck brands?

Kenworth (W900, T800), Peterbilt (379, 389), and Western Star (4900, 49X) are the dominant logging truck brands in North America. These models are built on heavy-duty frames suitable for the severe duty cycle of log hauling. Freightliner Cascadia is also used in some markets.

How much does it cost to start a logging truck business?

Starting a log hauling business requires the truck and trailer ($70,000–$380,000 depending on new vs. used and loader configuration), Class A CDL, commercial insurance ($1,200–$3,000/month), oversize/overweight permits, and working capital for the first 60–90 days of operations before revenue from timber contracts begins. Total startup costs typically range from $80,000 for a basic used setup to $400,000+ for a new self-loading configuration.
