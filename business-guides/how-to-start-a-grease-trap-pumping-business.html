How to Start a Grease Trap Pumping Business

Regular pumping is required by health departments and sewer authorities. This guide covers forming your business, securing disposal agreements, obtaining permits, purchasing or financing a grease vac truck, and building a restaurant customer base.

Overview

A grease trap pumping business removes fats, oils, and grease from restaurant and commercial kitchen grease traps. Grease traps prevent FOG from entering sewer lines; regular pumping is required by health departments and sewer authorities. Revenue comes from per-pump or recurring service contracts. Success depends on disposal agreements, permits, reliable equipment, and strong restaurant relationships. Many operators also offer septic pumping for additional revenue.

Customers and Revenue

Primary customers include restaurants, food service facilities, schools, hospitals, and commercial kitchens. Revenue from pumping fees ($150–$400+ per pump) and recurring service contracts. Building relationships with restaurant managers, property managers, and health department contacts drives referrals. Recurring contracts provide predictable income. Some jurisdictions require proof of pumping for permit renewal.

Equipment

Core equipment is a vacuum truck configured for grease/liquid waste. Tank capacity (500–3,000+ gallons) affects efficiency. See how much does a vac truck cost. Vac truck financing is available. New: $80,000–$200,000; used: $40,000–$120,000. Same equipment can often do septic and grease; disposal differs.

Typical Equipment Needed

  • Vacuum truck (grease/liquid waste)
  • Hoses, nozzles, pumping equipment
  • Spill containment and PPE
  • Scheduling and route software

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Grease trap pumpers must meet CDL and environmental permit requirements. See commercial truck license requirements.

CDL: Class A or Class B CDL if GVWR exceeds 26,000 lbs. Some smaller vac trucks may not require CDL–check state rules.

DOT: USDOT number if interstate. Many operations are intrastate.

State and local: Grease waste hauler permit, disposal site agreements, tank certification. Health department and sewer authority requirements vary.

Disclaimer: Licensing and permit requirements vary by state and county. Verify with your health department and environmental agency before operating.

Typical License Requirements

  • Class A or Class B CDL (if GVWR exceeds 26,000 lbs)
  • Grease waste hauler permit
  • Disposal site agreements
  • State business registration

Typical Startup Cost

Total startup: $70,000–$180,000 depending on truck, down payment, and operating reserve. Disposal agreements must be in place before operating. See average cost of commercial trucks.

Insurance

Commercial auto liability, cargo/pollution coverage, and general liability. Disposal facilities may require specific coverage. Environmental liability is important for liquid waste hauling.

Typical Insurance Needs

  • Commercial auto liability
  • Cargo/pollution coverage
  • General liability
  • Workers comp (if employees)

Financing

Vac truck financing is available from specialty commercial lenders. Down payment requirements vary by credit–strong credit and established businesses may qualify for low or no down payment; new businesses often need 20–30%. Proof of disposal agreements and restaurant contracts strengthens applications. Loan terms typically 60–72 months for new trucks, 24–60 months for used.

Common Mistakes When Starting This Type of Business

  • Underestimating insurance costs – Commercial auto, pollution/cargo, and liability can run $3,000–$8,000+ annually. Restaurant and facility contracts may require specific limits.
  • Choosing the wrong equipment – FOG disposal requires different tank certification than septic. Mixing grease and septic disposal violates facility agreements. Match vac truck specs to FOG hauling requirements.
  • Failing to obtain proper licensing – Disposal agreements with approved FOG facilities, wastewater hauler permits, and health department compliance must be in place before operating.
  • Undercapitalizing the business – Disposal fees and slow restaurant payment strain cash flow. Plan for 3–6 months of operating reserves.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to start a grease trap pumping business?

Startup costs typically range from $70,000 to $180,000 including grease vac truck, permits, disposal agreements, insurance, and operating capital.

Do I need permits?

Yes. Grease haulers typically need state/county permits, disposal site agreements, and possibly wastewater hauler licenses.

What's the difference between grease trap and septic pumping?

Grease traps collect FOG from restaurant kitchens. Septic tanks collect wastewater from homes. Different disposal requirements.

Can I start with one truck?

Yes. Many grease trap pumpers start with one vac truck serving restaurants and commercial kitchens.

Can I finance a grease vac truck as a new business?

Yes. Some lenders work with new grease trap pumping businesses. Down payment varies by credit.

Is a down payment always required?

No. Down payment varies by credit. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down.

Where does grease trap waste go?

Approved FOG disposal facilities, renderers, or wastewater treatment plants. Disposal agreements are required.

How often do restaurants need pumping?

Typically every 30–90 days. Health departments and sewer authorities may require documentation.
