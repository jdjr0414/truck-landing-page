How to Start a Towing Company

Starting a towing company requires equipment, insurance, permits, and contracts. This guide walks through the steps: forming your business, obtaining permits, purchasing or financing a tow truck, securing insurance, and landing AAA or insurance network contracts. Revenue comes from per-call fees, police rotation, and direct customers.

Key Takeaways

  • Tow trucks typically cost between $50k and $150k
  • Many require a Class B CDL
  • Financing terms commonly range from 36-60 months
  • Strong credit businesses may qualify with little or no down payment

Step-by-Step Overview

How to Start a Towing Company

  1. Form your business (LLC or corporation)
  2. Obtain local permits
  3. Purchase or finance a tow truck
  4. Get insurance
  5. Apply for AAA or insurance network contracts

Overview

A towing company provides roadside assistance, vehicle recovery, and impound services. Revenue streams include AAA/insurance network calls, police rotation, and direct customer work. Success depends on response time, equipment reliability, and contract relationships. Many operators start with one truck and scale as call volume grows.

Customers and Revenue

Primary customers: AAA members, insurance companies, police departments, and direct motorists. Revenue from per-call fees ($75–$200+ local), mileage, storage, and impound. AAA and insurance networks provide steady volume; police rotation adds predictable income. Building reputation for fast response and fair pricing drives repeat business.

Equipment

Core equipment is a tow truck—flatbed (versatile), wheel-lift (lower cost), or integrated (heavy recovery). See how much does a tow truck cost. Tow truck financing is available. Flatbeds suit AAA and insurance work. New: $60,000–$130,000; used: $30,000–$80,000.

Typical Equipment Needed

  • Tow truck (flatbed, wheel-lift, integrated, or rotator)
  • Winch, straps, chains
  • Storage lot (for impound)
  • Dispatch software

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Towing operators must meet CDL and local requirements. See commercial truck license requirements and do you need a CDL for a tow truck.

CDL: Tow trucks over 26,000 lbs GVWR typically require Class A or Class B CDL. Some light-duty wheel-lifts may not.

DOT: USDOT number if interstate. Many towing operations are intrastate.

State and local: Towing permits, impound licenses, business registration. Police rotation often requires specific permits.

OSHA and specialized: Generally not required. Recovery training recommended.

Disclaimer: Licensing requirements vary by state and locality. Verify with the FMCSA, your state DMV, and local authorities before operating.

Typical License Requirements

  • Class A or Class B CDL (if GVWR exceeds 26,000 lbs)
  • Local towing permit
  • Impound license (if applicable)
  • State business registration

Typical Startup Cost

Total startup: $50,000–$150,000 depending on new vs. used truck, down payment, and operating reserve. See average cost of commercial trucks for context.

Typical Timeline to Launch

  • Business formation: 1–2 weeks
  • CDL: 2–8 weeks if new; some light-duty tow trucks may not require CDL
  • Equipment purchase/financing: 1–2 weeks
  • Insurance and permits: 1–2 weeks
  • First revenue: Often within 4–10 weeks; AAA and police rotation can take longer

Insurance

Commercial auto liability, cargo insurance, and garage keepers. AAA and insurance networks often require $1M+ liability. Commercial insurance providers that specialize in towing and recovery offer competitive rates.

Typical Insurance Needs

  • Commercial auto liability ($1M+ for AAA/insurance)
  • Cargo insurance
  • Garage keepers legal liability
  • Workers comp (if employees)

Financing

Tow truck financing is available from specialty commercial lenders. Down payment varies by credit—strong credit may qualify for low or no down payment; new businesses often need 20–30%. Proof of AAA or insurance contracts strengthens applications. Loan terms typically 36–60 months.

Common Mistakes When Starting This Type of Business

  • Underestimating insurance costs – Commercial auto, cargo, and garage keepers can run $4,000–$12,000+ annually. AAA and insurance networks often require $1M+ liability. Budget for full coverage before applying for contracts.
  • Choosing the wrong equipment – A wheel-lift may not suit AAA work; a heavy-duty integrated truck may be overkill for light-duty. Match tow truck type (flatbed, wheel-lift, integrated) to your target market.
  • Failing to obtain proper licensing – CDL (if GVWR exceeds 26,000 lbs), local towing permits, and impound licenses must be in place. Operating without proper licensing risks contract rejection and fines.
  • Undercapitalizing the business – Slow payment from AAA and insurance networks (30–60 days) strains cash flow. Plan for 3–6 months of operating reserves.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to start a towing company?

Startup costs typically range from $50,000 to $150,000 including tow truck, insurance, permits, and operating capital.

What type of tow truck should I start with?

Flatbed tow trucks are versatile for light and medium duty. Wheel-lift is lower cost. Rotator tow trucks ($800k–$1.7M) suit heavy semi recovery. Choose based on your target market.

How do I get AAA or insurance contracts?

Apply through AAA's contractor program or insurance network. Requirements include insurance limits, response time, and geographic coverage.

Can I finance a tow truck as a new business?

Yes. Down payment varies by credit—strong credit may qualify for low or no down payment. Proof of contracts helps.

Do I need a CDL for a tow truck?

Tow trucks over 26,000 lbs typically require Class A or Class B CDL. Check state requirements.

Is a down payment always required for tow truck financing?

No. Down payment varies by credit. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down. New businesses often need 20–30%.

How much down payment for a tow truck as a new business?

Typically 20–30%. Proof of AAA or insurance contracts can reduce requirements. Down payment is not always required for strong credit.

Can I finance a used tow truck as a new business?

Yes. Used tow trucks ($30,000–$80,000) reduce startup cost. Down payment varies by credit.
