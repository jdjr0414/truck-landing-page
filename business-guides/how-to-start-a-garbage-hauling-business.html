  1. Form your business (LLC or corporation)
  2. Obtain required licenses and permits
  3. Purchase or finance equipment
  4. Get insurance
  5. Secure contracts or customers

Overview

A garbage hauling business collects and transports solid waste from residential, commercial, or municipal customers to landfills or transfer stations. Revenue comes from per-stop fees, container rental, and municipal contracts. Success depends on disposal agreements, route density, and equipment reliability. Municipal contracts provide steady volume; commercial and residential add margin.

Customers and Revenue

Primary customers: municipalities (contracts), commercial properties, residential customers, and HOAs. Revenue from per-stop fees ($15–$40+ monthly residential), container rental, and haul charges. Municipal contracts drive volume in many markets. Building route density reduces cost per stop. Commercial accounts provide higher revenue per stop.

Equipment

Core equipment: garbage truck (rear-load, side-load, or front-load). Truck type depends on route type. See how much does a garbage truck cost. Garbage truck financing is available. New: $150,000–$350,000; used: $60,000–$180,000. See waste management truck financing.

Typical Equipment Needed

  • Garbage truck (rear-load, side-load, or front-load)
  • Containers (residential or commercial)
  • Route software

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Garbage haulers must meet CDL and local permits. See commercial truck license requirements.

CDL: Garbage trucks over 26,000 lbs GVWR typically require Class A or Class B CDL.

DOT: USDOT number if interstate. Many operations are intrastate.

State and local: Solid waste hauler permits, disposal agreements, municipal licensing. EPA and state environmental regulations apply to waste handling. Zoning for yard.

OSHA and specialized: Generally not required. Safety training recommended.

Disclaimer: Licensing requirements vary by state and locality. Verify with the FMCSA, EPA (for waste handling), your state DMV, and local authorities before operating.

Typical License Requirements

  • Class A or Class B CDL
  • Solid waste hauler permit
  • Disposal agreements
  • Municipal license (if applicable)

Insurance

Commercial auto liability, general liability. Municipal contracts often require $1M+ limits. Commercial insurance providers that specialize in waste hauling can help meet contract requirements. Workers comp if you have employees.

Typical Insurance Needs

  • Commercial auto liability ($1M+ for municipal)
  • General liability
  • Workers comp (if employees)

Financing

Garbage truck financing is available from specialty commercial lenders. Down payment varies by credit—strong credit may qualify for low or no down payment; new businesses often need 20–30%. Proof of municipal contracts or route pipeline helps. Loan terms typically 60–72 months.

Common Mistakes When Starting This Type of Business

  • Underestimating insurance costs – Commercial auto, cargo, and liability can run $5,000–$15,000+ annually. Municipal contracts often require $1M+ limits. Budget for full coverage before bidding.
  • Choosing the wrong equipment – Rear-load, side-load, and front-load trucks serve different route types. Buying the wrong truck type for your target route (residential vs. commercial) hurts efficiency.
  • Failing to obtain proper licensing – Disposal agreements, transfer station access, and municipal permits must be secured. Operating without proper licensing risks contract rejection and fines.
  • Undercapitalizing the business – Tipping fees, container inventory, and municipal payment cycles (30–90 days) strain cash flow. Plan for 6+ months of operating reserves.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to start a garbage hauling business?

Startup costs typically range from $80,000 to $350,000 including garbage truck, disposal agreements, insurance, and operating capital.

Who hires garbage hauling companies?

Municipalities, commercial properties, residential customers, and HOAs.

Can I finance a garbage truck as a new business?

Yes. Down payment varies by credit—strong credit may qualify for low or no down payment. Proof of contracts helps.

Do I need permits for garbage hauling?

Yes. Solid waste hauler permits, disposal agreements, and possibly municipal licensing.

Is a down payment always required for garbage truck financing?

No. Down payment varies by credit. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down. New businesses often need 20–30%.

How much down payment for a garbage truck as a new business?

Typically 20–30%. Proof of municipal or route contracts can reduce requirements. Down payment is not always required for strong credit.

Can I finance a used garbage truck as a new business?

Yes. Used garbage trucks can reduce startup cost. Down payment varies by credit.
