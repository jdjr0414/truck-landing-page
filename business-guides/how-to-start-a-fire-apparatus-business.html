  1. Form your business (LLC or corporation)
  2. Obtain required licenses and permits
  3. Purchase or finance equipment
  4. Get insurance
  5. Secure contracts or customers

Overview

A fire apparatus business owns and operates private fire trucks–brush trucks, water tenders, and sometimes pumpers–for contract firefighting. Revenue comes from municipal standby agreements, state wildfire contracts, private landowner protection, industrial fire brigades, and event standby. This is a niche market: customers are governments, agencies, and large landowners. Success depends on fire certifications, CDL compliance, reliable equipment, and relationships with fire agencies and contract administrators.

Customers and Revenue

Primary customers: municipalities (standby and mutual aid), state forestry and wildfire agencies (contract firefighting), private landowners and ranches, industrial facilities, and event venues. Revenue from standby fees, per-call response, wildfire contract rates, and annual protection agreements. Wildfire season drives demand in western states. Municipal contracts may be competitive–prequalification and bonding required.

Equipment

Core equipment: brush truck (4x4 with pump and tank for wildland fire attack) or water tender (tanker to supply water to pumpers). See fire truck financing. Brush truck: $80,000–$200,000+. Water tender: $100,000–$250,000+. Used apparatus can reduce cost. Full pumpers cost $300,000–$700,000+ and are typically for larger operations. CDL required for vehicles over 26,000 lbs GVWR.

Typical Equipment Needed

  • Brush truck (wildland fire attack)
  • Water tender / tanker (water supply)
  • Pump, hose, and nozzles
  • Firefighting PPE and tools
  • Radio and communications

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Fire apparatus businesses must meet CDL and fire certification requirements. See commercial truck license requirements.

CDL: Required for brush trucks and water tenders over 26,000 lbs GVWR. Class A for tractor-trailer tankers; Class B for single-unit apparatus.

Fire certifications: Firefighter I/II, pump operator, and wildland fire certifications (e.g., NWCG) often required by contracts. State and agency requirements vary.

DOT: USDOT number if interstate. Many contract firefighting is intrastate.

Business registration: LLC or corporation, insurance, and bonding for municipal work.

Disclaimer: Licensing and certification requirements vary by state and contract. Verify with fire agencies and contract administrators before operating.

Typical License Requirements

  • Class A or Class B CDL
  • Firefighter I/II or equivalent
  • Pump operator certification
  • Wildland fire certifications (for wildfire contracts)
  • State business registration

Insurance

Commercial auto liability, general liability, and equipment coverage. Municipal and wildfire contracts may require specific coverage limits. Workers comp if you have employees.

Typical Insurance Needs

  • Commercial auto liability
  • General liability
  • Equipment / apparatus coverage
  • Workers comp (if employees)

Financing

Fire truck financing is available from specialty commercial lenders. Brush trucks and water tenders are niche–fewer lenders than standard trucks. Down payment varies by credit–strong credit may qualify for low or no down payment; new businesses often need 20–30%. See commercial vehicle financing for options. Loan terms typically 60–84 months for fire apparatus.

Common Mistakes When Starting This Type of Business

  • Underestimating insurance costs – Fire apparatus insurance is specialized and can be costly. Contract requirements often specify minimum limits. Budget for full coverage before bidding.
  • Choosing the wrong equipment – Brush trucks, water tenders, and attack trucks serve different contract needs. Buying apparatus that doesn't match contract specifications limits opportunities.
  • Failing to obtain proper licensing – Fire certifications, pump and tank certification, and contract qualifications must be in place. Operating without required credentials risks contract rejection.
  • Undercapitalizing the business – Apparatus is expensive and maintenance is high. Wildfire seasonality concentrates revenue in summer. Plan for 6+ months of operating reserves.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to start a fire apparatus business?

Startup costs typically range from $100,000 to $300,000+ including brush truck or water tender, fire certifications, insurance, and working capital.

Do I need a CDL for a fire apparatus business?

Yes for brush trucks and water tenders over 26,000 lbs GVWR. Class A or Class B CDL is typically required.

What fire certifications do I need?

Firefighter I/II, pump operator, and possibly wildland fire certifications. Requirements vary by contract and jurisdiction.

Can I finance a brush truck or water tender as a new business?

Yes. Fire truck financing is available. Down payment varies by credit–new businesses often need 20–30%.

Is a down payment always required for fire truck financing?

No. Down payment varies by credit. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down. New businesses often need 20–30%.

Who are the main customers for private fire apparatus?

Municipalities, state forestry/wildfire agencies, private landowners, industrial facilities, and event venues.

What is the difference between a brush truck and a water tender?

Brush trucks are 4x4 vehicles with pump and tank for wildland fire attack. Water tenders haul water to supply pumpers.

How much does a brush truck or water tender cost?

Brush truck: $80,000–$200,000+. Water tender: $100,000–$250,000+. Used apparatus can reduce cost. Down payment varies by credit when financing.
