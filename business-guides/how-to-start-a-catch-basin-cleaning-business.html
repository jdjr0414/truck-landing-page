How to Start a Catch Basin Cleaning Business

A catch basin cleaning business cleans storm drain inlets (catch basins) for municipalities, commercial properties, and HOAs. Catch basins collect stormwater and debris; regular cleaning prevents clogging and improves water quality. This guide covers forming your business, obtaining a CDL and disposal agreements, purchasing or financing a vac truck, and bidding on municipal and commercial contracts.

Overview

A catch basin cleaning business uses vacuum trucks to remove sediment, debris, and pollutants from storm drain inlets. Municipalities require catch basin cleaning for stormwater compliance. Commercial properties and HOAs also need periodic cleaning. Success depends on reliable equipment, disposal agreements, and efficient routing. Municipal contracts often provide steady, multi-year revenue.

Customers and Revenue

Primary customers include municipalities, county public works, commercial property managers, HOAs, and industrial facilities. Revenue comes from per-basin or per-contract rates. Municipal contracts may be per-basin ($15–$50+) or lump-sum. Building relationships with public works directors and stormwater managers secures work. MS4 permit compliance drives municipal demand.

Equipment

Core equipment is a vacuum truck with catch basin cleaning capability–hose, nozzle, and debris tank. Tank capacity affects efficiency. See how much does a vac truck cost. Vac truck financing is available. New: $120,000–$250,000; used: $60,000–$150,000.

Typical Equipment Needed

  • Vacuum truck (catch basin / combination unit)
  • Cleaning hose and nozzle
  • Debris/sediment tank
  • Route management software

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Catch basin cleaners must meet CDL and environmental requirements. See commercial truck license requirements.

CDL: Class A or Class B CDL depending on GVWR. Most vac trucks exceed 26,000 lbs.

DOT: USDOT number if interstate. Many operations are intrastate.

State and local: Business registration. Stormwater or wastewater hauler permits. Disposal site agreements with approved facilities. Some jurisdictions require specific certifications.

Disclaimer: Licensing and permit requirements vary by state and jurisdiction. Verify with your state environmental agency and local public works before operating.

Typical License Requirements

  • Class A or Class B CDL
  • Disposal site agreements
  • Stormwater/wastewater hauler permit (if required)
  • State business registration

Typical Startup Cost

Total startup: $80,000–$250,000 depending on truck, down payment, and operating reserve. Disposal agreements must be in place before operating. See average cost of commercial trucks.

Insurance

Commercial auto liability is mandatory. Municipal contracts often require $1M or higher limits. General liability protects against property damage claims. Workers compensation is required if you have employees.

Typical Insurance Needs

  • Commercial auto liability ($1M+ common)
  • General liability
  • Workers comp (if employees)

Financing

Vac truck financing is available from specialty commercial lenders. Down payment requirements vary by credit–strong credit and established businesses may qualify for low or no down payment; new businesses often need 20–30%. Proof of municipal or commercial contracts strengthens applications. Loan terms typically 60–72 months for new trucks, 24–60 months for used.

Common Mistakes

Avoid operating without disposal agreements–you cannot legally dispose of catch basin waste without approved facility agreements. Don't bid on municipal contracts without understanding MS4 and stormwater requirements. Failing to track basins cleaned and document disposal creates compliance risk. Underestimating disposal costs reduces margins. Ignoring traffic control and work zone safety creates liability.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to start a catch basin cleaning business?

Startup costs typically range from $80,000 to $250,000 including vac truck, insurance, permits, and operating capital.

Do I need a CDL?

Yes. Vac trucks typically require Class A or Class B CDL depending on GVWR.

What is a catch basin?

A catch basin (storm drain inlet) collects stormwater and debris. Cleaning removes sediment to prevent clogging and improve water quality.

Can I start with one truck?

Yes. Many catch basin cleaners start with one vac truck serving municipal and commercial contracts.

Can I finance a vac truck as a new business?

Yes. Some lenders work with new catch basin cleaning businesses. Down payment varies by credit.

Is a down payment always required?

No. Down payment varies by credit. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down.

Where do I dispose of catch basin waste?

Approved disposal facilities, wastewater treatment plants, or landfills. Disposal agreements are required.

How do I get municipal contracts?

Monitor city and county RFPs for stormwater maintenance. Bid competitively. Some municipalities have multi-year contracts.
