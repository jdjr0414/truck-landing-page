Step-by-Step Overview

How to Start a Mobile Service Truck Business

  1. Form your business (LLC or corporation)
  2. Obtain required licenses and permits
  3. Purchase or finance equipment
  4. Get insurance
  5. Secure contracts or customers

Overview

A mobile service truck business provides on-site repair and maintenance–HVAC, plumbing, equipment repair, fleet maintenance, welding, or hydraulic service. Revenue comes from hourly rates, service call fees, and parts markup. Success depends on technical skills, equipment reliability, and customer relationships. Many operators start solo and scale with additional trucks.

Customers and Revenue

Primary customers: fleet managers, contractors, property managers, manufacturers, and direct consumers. Revenue from hourly rates ($75–$150+ per hour), service call fees ($75–$150+), and parts. Fleet maintenance contracts provide recurring revenue. Building relationships with dispatchers and facility managers drives growth.

Equipment

Core equipment: service body truck with storage, tools, and possibly crane or welder. Service truck financing and utility truck financing are available. New: $40,000–$100,000; used: $20,000–$60,000. Tool investment varies by niche. See utility truck financing for related equipment.

Typical Equipment Needed

  • Service body truck
  • Hand and power tools
  • Parts inventory
  • Possibly crane, welder, or compressor

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Mobile service operators must meet state and local requirements. See commercial truck license requirements.

CDL: Service trucks under 26,001 lbs GVWR may not require CDL. Over 26,001 lbs typically requires Class B CDL.

DOT: USDOT number if interstate. Many operations are intrastate.

State and local: Business registration. Trade licenses (HVAC, plumbing, electrical) may apply depending on niche.

OSHA and specialized: Trade-specific certifications (EPA for HVAC, etc.). Generally not required for equipment repair.

Disclaimer: Licensing requirements vary by state and trade. Verify with your state and local authorities before operating.

Typical License Requirements

  • Class B CDL (if GVWR exceeds 26,000 lbs)
  • Trade license (if applicable)
  • State business registration

Insurance

Commercial auto liability, general liability. Fleet and contractor clients may require $1M+ limits. Workers comp if you have employees.

Typical Insurance Needs

  • Commercial auto liability
  • General liability
  • Workers comp (if employees)

Financing

Service truck financing is available from specialty commercial lenders. Down payment varies by credit–strong credit may qualify for low or no down payment; new businesses often need 20–30%. Proof of contracts or revenue helps. Loan terms typically 36–60 months.

Common Mistakes

Starting without a defined niche–spreading too thin limits expertise. Undercapitalization; tools and parts tie up cash. Underpricing–hourly rates must cover truck, tools, and overhead. Failing to get trade licenses when required. Skipping insurance verification for fleet contracts.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to start a mobile service truck business?

Startup costs typically range from $30,000 to $120,000 including service truck, tools, insurance, and operating capital.

What services can a mobile service truck provide?

HVAC repair, plumbing, equipment repair, fleet maintenance, welding, hydraulic repair, and general mobile service.

Can I finance a service truck as a new business?

Yes. Down payment varies by credit–strong credit may qualify for low or no down payment. Proof of contracts helps.

Do I need a CDL for a service truck?

Service trucks under 26,001 lbs may not require CDL. Over 26,001 lbs typically requires Class B CDL.

Is a down payment always required for service truck financing?

No. Down payment varies by credit. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down. New businesses often need 20–30%.

How much down payment for a service truck as a new business?

Typically 20–30%. Proof of contracts can reduce requirements. Down payment is not always required for strong credit.

Can I finance a used service truck as a new business?

Yes. Used service trucks can reduce startup cost. Down payment varies by credit.
