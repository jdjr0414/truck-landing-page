How to Start a Trucking Company

Starting a trucking company requires operating authority, insurance, equipment, and freight. This guide walks through the steps: forming your business, obtaining FMCSA authority, securing insurance, purchasing or financing a tractor, and finding loads. Owner-operators and fleet starters both use this process.

Quick Answer

To start a trucking company: form an LLC, obtain FMCSA operating authority (MC number), get insurance, purchase or finance a tractor, and find freight via load boards or carrier agreements. Startup costs: $15,000–$50,000+ before equipment. Tractors: $50,000–$200,000. See startup trucking business financing.

Step 1: Form Your Business

Register as an LLC or corporation. Obtain an EIN. Open a business bank account. Establish separate business credit.

Step 2: Obtain Operating Authority

For-hire interstate hauling requires FMCSA operating authority (MC number). Process includes USDOT number, insurance filings, and process agent. Allow several weeks for approval.

Step 3: Insurance

Primary liability ($750K–$1M+), cargo insurance, and physical damage. New authorities often require higher deposits. Shop multiple carriers.

Step 4: Purchase or Finance a Tractor

New tractors: $120,000–$200,000. Used: $50,000–$120,000. See how much does a semi truck cost. Semi truck financing for new companies often requires 20–30% down. See best truck for starting trucking company.

Load boards, freight brokers, carrier agreements, and direct shipper relationships. Consider leasing to an established carrier (lease-on) to build revenue history before going independent.

Common Questions

What credit score do I need to finance a truck as a new trucking company?

Many lenders prefer 650+ for competitive terms. Some work with 580–650 with higher down payments (25–30%). Carrier agreements help.

How much down payment for a truck as a new trucking company?

Typically 20–30%. First-time truck buyers often need more. Proof of carrier agreements or load history can reduce requirements.

Can startup trucking companies get financing?

Yes. Some lenders work with new trucking companies. Expect 20–30% down and proof of contracts or carrier agreements.

How long are truck loan terms for new trucking companies?

New tractors: 60–84 months. Used: 36–60 months. New companies may face stricter terms until they establish revenue.

How fast can new trucking companies get financing approved?

Simple applications: 1–3 business days. Complex deals: 1–2 weeks. Carrier agreements and complete docs speed the process.

Can I finance a used truck as a new trucking company?

Yes. Used tractors reduce startup cost ($50,000–$120,000 vs $120,000–$200,000 new). Expect 20–30% down. Many starters buy used.

What documentation do new trucking companies need?

Operating authority, insurance proof, business formation docs, tax returns (if any), bank statements, carrier agreements, and equipment details.

How much does it cost to start a trucking company?

Startup costs typically range from $15,000 to $50,000+ for authority, insurance, and deposits—before equipment. Tractors cost $120,000–$200,000 new or $50,000–$120,000 used.

Do I need operating authority to start a trucking company?

For-hire interstate hauling requires FMCSA operating authority (MC number). Intrastate and private carriage may have different requirements.

What is the best truck for starting a trucking company?

Depends on freight type. Day cabs for regional; sleepers for OTR. Used tractors can reduce startup cost.
