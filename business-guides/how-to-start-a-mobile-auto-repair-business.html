How to Start a Mobile Auto Repair Business

A mobile auto repair business brings mechanic services to the customer–fleet yards, dealerships, or residential. This guide covers forming your business, obtaining certifications, purchasing or financing a service truck, securing insurance, and building fleet and consumer relationships. Revenue comes from labor and parts markup.

Overview

A mobile auto repair business performs mechanic services at the customer's location–fleet yards, dealerships, or homes. Services include brakes, oil changes, diagnostics, batteries, and minor repairs. Success depends on skills, reliable equipment, and strong fleet or consumer relationships. Many operators focus on fleet maintenance for steady revenue.

Customers and Revenue

Primary customers include fleet managers, trucking companies, dealerships, and consumers. Revenue from labor rates ($75–$150/hr) and parts markup. Fleet contracts provide recurring revenue. Building relationships with fleet managers and offering preventive maintenance secures work.

Equipment

Core equipment is a service truck or van with tool storage, compressor, and diagnostic equipment. Service truck financing is available. New: $45,000–$90,000; used: $25,000–$55,000.

Typical Equipment Needed

  • Service truck or van
  • Tool storage and organization
  • Air compressor
  • Diagnostic scanner

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Requirements vary by state. See commercial truck license requirements.

CDL: Usually not required for service trucks under 26,000 lbs GVWR.

Mechanic license: Some states require no formal license. ASE certifications build credibility.

State and local: Business registration. Some localities require home occupation permits.

Disclaimer: Licensing requirements vary by state. Verify with your state before operating.

Typical License Requirements

  • CDL usually not required
  • ASE certifications (recommended)
  • State business registration

Insurance

Commercial auto liability and general liability. Fleet contracts may require $1M+. Workers comp if you have employees.

Typical Insurance Needs

  • Commercial auto liability
  • General liability
  • Workers comp (if employees)

Financing

Service truck financing is available. Down payment varies by credit. Proof of fleet or dealer contracts strengthens applications.

Common Mistakes

Avoid underinvesting in tools–quality equipment improves efficiency. Don't skip insurance; fleet contracts require it. Failing to build fleet relationships before buying equipment creates revenue risk. Underpricing labor reduces margins.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to start a mobile auto repair business?

Startup costs typically range from $40,000 to $100,000 including service truck, tools, insurance, and operating capital.

Do I need a CDL?

Usually not. Service trucks under 26,000 lbs typically do not require CDL.

Do I need a mechanic license?

Requirements vary by state. ASE certifications build credibility.

Can I start with one truck?

Yes. Many mobile mechanics start with one service truck.

Can I finance a service truck as a new business?

Yes. Some lenders work with new mobile repair businesses. Down payment varies by credit.

Is a down payment always required?

No. Down payment varies by credit. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down.

How do I get fleet contracts?

Contact fleet managers, trucking companies, and dealerships. Offer preventive maintenance and diagnostics.

What services can mobile mechanics offer?

Brakes, oil changes, diagnostics, batteries, minor repairs. Scope depends on tools and expertise.
