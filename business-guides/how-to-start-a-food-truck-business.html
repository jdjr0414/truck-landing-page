How to Start a Food Truck Business

Starting a food truck business requires a commercial vehicle (step van or trailer), kitchen equipment, health department permits, and vending licenses. This guide walks through forming your business, obtaining health and vending permits, purchasing or financing a food truck or step van, and building revenue through events, street vending, and catering. Most food trucks do not require a CDL.

Overview

A food truck business operates a mobile kitchen—typically a step van, box truck, or trailer—to sell prepared food at events, street locations, and private catering gigs. Revenue comes from direct sales, event fees, and catering contracts. Success depends on permits, food quality, location strategy, and equipment reliability. Many operators start with a single truck and expand to multiple units or a brick-and-mortar location.

Customers and Revenue

Primary revenue streams: street vending (lunch rushes, downtown spots), events (festivals, farmers markets, corporate gatherings), and catering (private parties, weddings, corporate lunches). Event bookings often provide higher margins and predictable income. Street vending requires consistent locations and permits. Catering builds recurring relationships. Diversifying across all three reduces weather and seasonal risk.

Equipment

Core equipment is a food truck or step van outfitted with a commercial kitchen. Food trucks are often step vans or box trucks converted for cooking. New or custom builds: $60,000–$150,000; used conversions: $30,000–$80,000. Kitchen equipment includes grills, fryers, refrigeration, ventilation, and prep stations. See box truck financing for step vans and commercial vehicles—there is no dedicated food truck financing page, but box truck and step van financing applies.

Typical Equipment Needed

  • Food truck, step van, or trailer
  • Commercial cooking equipment (grill, fryer, flat-top)
  • Refrigeration and freezer
  • Ventilation and exhaust system
  • Point-of-sale and payment systems

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Food truck operators must meet health department and local vending requirements. See commercial truck license requirements for vehicle-related rules.

CDL: Usually not required. Most food trucks and step vans are under 26,000 lbs GVWR. Trailers under 10,000 lbs typically do not require CDL. Verify your vehicle weight and state rules.

Health department: Mobile food unit permit, food handler certification, and commissary agreement (where you store, prep, or clean). Health inspections are required before operating.

Vending permits: Street vending permits, event permits, and zone-specific licenses. Many cities limit where and when food trucks can operate.

State and local: Business registration, sales tax permit, and possibly a food service license. Fire department may require hood suppression inspection.

Disclaimer: Licensing and permit requirements vary by state, county, and city. Verify with your health department and local government before operating.

Insurance

Commercial auto liability, general liability, and product liability for food service. Event organizers and landlords often require certificates of insurance. Workers comp if you have employees.

Typical Insurance Needs

  • Commercial auto liability
  • General liability
  • Product liability (food)
  • Workers comp (if employees)

Financing

Box truck financing and step van financing apply to food trucks—there is no dedicated food truck financing product, but commercial lenders finance step vans and box trucks used as mobile kitchens. Down payment varies by credit—strong credit may qualify for low or no down payment; new businesses often need 20–30%. Business plan and menu concept help. Loan terms typically 36–60 months.

Common Mistakes When Starting This Type of Business

  • Underestimating insurance costs – Commercial auto, general liability, and product liability can run $3,000–$8,000+ annually. Events and venues often require certificates.
  • Choosing the wrong equipment – Step van vs. trailer, kitchen layout, and ventilation must match your concept and health department requirements. Wrong setup limits where you can operate.
  • Failing to obtain proper licensing – Health department permits, commissary agreements, and vending zone approvals must be secured before operating. Starting without permits risks shutdowns.
  • Undercapitalizing the business – Food inventory, permits, and slow event payouts strain cash flow. Plan for 3–6 months of operating reserves.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to start a food truck business?

Startup costs typically range from $50,000 to $180,000 including food truck or step van ($30,000–$150,000), kitchen equipment, permits, insurance, and working capital.

Do I need a CDL for a food truck?

Usually no. Most food trucks and step vans are under 26,000 lbs GVWR and do not require a CDL. Verify your vehicle weight and state requirements.

What permits do I need for a food truck?

Health department mobile food unit permit, food handler certification, vending permits, and local business licenses. Commissary agreement is often required.

Can I finance a food truck as a new business?

Yes. Box truck and step van financing is available. Down payment varies by credit—strong credit may qualify for low or no down payment.

Is a down payment always required for food truck financing?

No. Down payment varies by credit. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down. New businesses often need 20–30%.

What is the best business model for a food truck?

Events, street vending, and catering. Diversifying across all three reduces weather and location risk.

Do I need a commissary for a food truck?

Most health departments require a commissary agreement—a licensed kitchen where you store, prep, or clean. Requirements vary by jurisdiction.

Can I use a step van for a food truck?

Yes. Step vans are common food truck platforms. They are commercial vehicles and qualify for box truck financing.
