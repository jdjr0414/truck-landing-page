How to Start a Vacuum Excavator Business

A vacuum excavator business uses vacuum trucks to perform non-destructive excavation–potholing, daylighting, and soft digging–near buried utilities. Unlike hydro excavation, vacuum excavation uses air and vacuum only (dry) or minimal water. This guide covers forming your business, obtaining a CDL, purchasing or financing a vacuum excavator truck, and building relationships with utilities and contractors.

  • Many require a Class B CDL

Overview

A vacuum excavator business performs non-destructive excavation using vacuum trucks. Air and vacuum loosen soil; the material is conveyed into a debris tank. Unlike hydro excavation, which uses pressurized water, vacuum excavation is "dry" or uses minimal water–well-suited for certain soils and environments. Applications include potholing, daylighting, slot trenching, and cleaning around utilities. Utilities, telecom, and excavation contractors are primary customers.

Customers and Revenue

Primary customers include electric and gas utilities, telecom companies, water and sewer agencies, excavation contractors, and pipeline companies. Revenue comes from per-hour or per-hole rates. Vacuum excavation rates typically $150–$350+ per hour depending on region and application. Building relationships with utility locators and project managers secures steady work. Demand is driven by utility construction and maintenance.

Equipment

Core equipment is a vacuum excavator truck–a vac truck with excavation capability. Dry systems use air; combination units can add water. See how much does a vac truck cost. Vacuum excavator truck financing and vac truck financing are available. New: $150,000–$300,000; used: $80,000–$180,000.

Typical Equipment Needed

  • Vacuum excavator truck (dry or combination)
  • Excavation wands and hoses
  • Debris tank (typically 10–20 cubic yards)
  • GPS and job management software

Licensing and Regulatory Requirements

Vacuum excavator operators must meet CDL and DOT requirements. See commercial truck license requirements.

CDL: Class A or Class B CDL depending on GVWR. Most vacuum excavators exceed 26,000 lbs.

DOT: USDOT number if interstate. Many operations are intrastate.

State and local: Business registration. 811/call-before-you-dig compliance required for excavation near utilities. Confined space training may apply for some work.

Disclaimer: Licensing requirements vary by state and operation type. Verify with your state DMV and DOT before operating.

Typical License Requirements

  • Class A or Class B CDL
  • USDOT number (interstate)
  • 811/call-before-you-dig compliance
  • State business registration

Insurance

Commercial auto liability is mandatory. Utility and contractor agreements often require $1M or higher limits. General liability protects against excavation damage claims. Workers compensation is required if you have employees.

Typical Insurance Needs

  • Commercial auto liability ($1M+ common)
  • General liability
  • Workers comp (if employees)

Financing

Vacuum excavator truck financing and vac truck financing are available from specialty commercial lenders. Down payment requirements vary by credit–strong credit and established businesses may qualify for low or no down payment; new businesses often need 20–30%. Proof of utility or contractor contracts strengthens applications. Loan terms typically 60–72 months for new equipment, 24–60 months for used.

Common Mistakes

Avoid confusing vacuum excavation with hydro excavation–equipment and applications differ. Don't skip 811/call-before-you-dig; hitting utilities causes damage and liability. Failing to secure disposal agreements for excavated material creates operational risk. Overpaying for used equipment without inspection leads to costly repairs. Ignoring utility contractor safety and PPE requirements creates job site risk.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to start a vacuum excavator business?

Startup costs typically range from $100,000 to $300,000 including vacuum excavator truck, insurance, permits, and operating capital.

What's the difference between vacuum and hydro excavation?

Vacuum excavation uses air and vacuum (dry). Hydro excavation uses pressurized water and vacuum. Both are used for potholing near utilities.

Do I need a CDL?

Yes. Vacuum excavator trucks typically require Class A or Class B CDL depending on GVWR.

Can I start with one truck?

Yes. Many vacuum excavation operators start with one truck serving utilities and contractors.

Can I finance a vacuum excavator as a new business?

Yes. Some lenders work with new vacuum excavation businesses. Down payment varies by credit.

Is a down payment always required?

No. Down payment varies by credit. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down.

Who hires vacuum excavation contractors?

Utilities, telecom, excavation contractors, municipalities, and pipeline companies. Used for potholing and daylighting.

What permits do I need?

CDL, DOT if interstate, state business registration. 811 compliance required for excavation near utilities.
