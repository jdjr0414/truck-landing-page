Do You Need a CDL for a Tow Truck?

Tow trucks often require a Class B CDL when GVWR exceeds 26,000 lbs. Heavy wreckers and integrated tow trucks typically exceed this threshold. Light-duty tow trucks may not require a CDL. Some states require tow operator licensing in addition to CDL. This guide covers CDL requirements and state-specific rules.

Quick Answer

Heavy wreckers and tow trucks over 26,000 pounds GVWR typically require a Class B CDL. Light-duty tow trucks may not require a CDL. Tow operator licensing may apply in some states. DOT registration is typically required for interstate operations. Rules vary by state–verify with the FMCSA and your state motor vehicle agency.

When Is a CDL Required for a Tow Truck?

Medium and heavy-duty tow trucks–including flatbeds, wheel-lift, and integrated wreckers–typically exceed 26,000 pounds GVWR and require a Class B CDL. Light-duty tow trucks (e.g., light-duty flatbeds under 26,000 lbs) may not require a CDL in many states. Check the manufacturer's GVWR for your specific unit.
