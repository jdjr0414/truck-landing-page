How Long Do Truck Loans Last?

Truck loan terms typically range from 3 to 7 years. New semi tractors often qualify for 4–6 years (sometimes up to 7); used tractors 3–5 years. Vocational trucks and specialty equipment may have shorter terms based on useful life. Longer terms mean lower monthly payments but more total interest. This guide covers typical terms by equipment type.

Key Takeaways

  • Commercial trucks typically cost between $3 and $7
  • Financing terms commonly range from 60-84 months
  • Strong credit businesses may qualify with little or no down payment

Common Questions

What credit score affects truck loan terms?

Terms are tied to equipment, not credit. Lower credit may get shorter terms. New equipment qualifies for longer terms than used.

How does down payment affect loan term?

Down payment affects rate and approval, not typically term length. Terms are tied to equipment useful life.

Can startups get longer truck loan terms?

New equipment may qualify for 60–84 months regardless of startup status. Used equipment: 24–60 months. Startups may face stricter terms.

What is the typical term for a semi truck loan?

4–6 years for new tractors. 3–5 years for used. Some lenders offer up to 7 years for new equipment.

How fast can I get a truck loan with a specific term?

Approval speed is independent of term. Simple applications: 1–3 business days. Term is selected at application.

Do used trucks have shorter loan terms?

Yes. Used trucks typically 24–60 months. New trucks: 60–84 months. Terms match remaining useful life.

What documents affect loan term?

Equipment details (age, mileage, condition) determine max term. Lenders won't finance longer than useful life.

What does loan term cost?

Longer terms = lower monthly payment, more total interest. Shorter terms = higher payment, less interest. Compare total cost.

Can I get a 7-year truck loan?

Some lenders offer 7-year terms for new tractors. Used equipment typically has shorter max terms.

What term length is best for a truck loan?

Match term to equipment life and cash flow. Don't finance longer than useful life. Balance payment with total cost.
