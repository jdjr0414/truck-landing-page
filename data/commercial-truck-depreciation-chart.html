Commercial Truck Depreciation Chart

Commercial trucks depreciate over time. Semi tractors typically lose 15–25% in year one, then 10–15% annually. Vocational and specialty trucks vary by type and use. This page summarizes typical depreciation patterns and factors that affect resale value.

Common Questions

What credit score do I need for truck financing?

Many lenders prefer 650+ for competitive terms. Depreciation affects collateral value, which lenders consider.

How much down payment for a truck?

Typically 10–30%. Depreciation affects used truck value. Lenders may require more down on older equipment.

Can startups finance trucks with high depreciation?

Yes. Used trucks have already depreciated, reducing cost. New trucks depreciate fastest in years 1–2.

How long are truck loan terms?

New: 60–84 months. Used: 24–60 months. Terms should not exceed useful life. Depreciation affects collateral.

How fast can I get truck financing?

Simple applications: 1–3 business days. Lenders consider depreciation when valuing collateral.

Does depreciation affect used truck financing?

Yes. Older trucks have depreciated more. Lenders may offer shorter terms and lower advance rates for equipment with high depreciation.

What documentation do I need for truck financing?

Tax returns, bank statements, P&L, and equipment details. Mileage and condition affect depreciation and valuation.

How much does a semi truck depreciate per year?

Semi tractors typically lose 15–25% in year one, then 10–15% annually.

Do vocational trucks depreciate faster than semis?

Depends on type. Heavy-use vocational may depreciate faster. Well-maintained specialty units can hold value.

What affects commercial truck depreciation?

Mileage, hours, condition, make/model, emissions tier, and market demand.
