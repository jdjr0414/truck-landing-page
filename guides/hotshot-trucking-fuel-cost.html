Definition

Hotshot trucking fuel cost is the expense of diesel or gasoline for a one-ton pickup towing a gooseneck trailer. It is typically the largest variable operating cost. Cost per mile = fuel price per gallon ÷ MPG. See hotshot trucking profit per load and commercial truck operating costs.

Cost Per Mile

Formula: diesel price per gallon ÷ MPG = cost per mile. Example: $4/gal ÷ 10 MPG = $0.40/mile. Diesel prices vary by region and season. Include deadhead (empty) miles in your estimate—empty miles still burn fuel. A 500-mile round trip with 200 deadhead miles means 700 total miles of fuel cost. Fuel cards can offer discounts and simplify recordkeeping for IFTA. Track fuel purchases by state for IFTA reporting. Use a fuel log or app for accuracy. See what is IFTA.

Diesel vs Gas

Diesel typically offers better MPG when towing—often 2–4 MPG better than comparable gas engines under load. Gas trucks have lower upfront cost but higher fuel cost per mile when towing. For high-mileage hotshot work, diesel usually pays off over time. Gas can make sense for lower-mileage or regional work. Most hotshot operators prefer diesel for long hauls. Diesel engines also tend to last longer under heavy use. See best truck for hotshot trucking.

Fuel Cards and IFTA

Fuel cards can offer per-gallon discounts and simplify IFTA recordkeeping. Many report fuel purchases by state automatically. Compare card programs—discounts and fees vary. IFTA requires quarterly reporting of miles and fuel by jurisdiction. Keep receipts and mileage logs. Accurate records simplify quarterly IFTA filing. File on time to avoid penalties. Deadhead miles count toward IFTA. See what is IFTA.

Reducing Fuel Cost

Minimize deadhead—empty miles burn fuel without revenue. Plan routes to reduce unnecessary miles. Maintain tires (proper inflation improves MPG) and engine (dirty filters reduce efficiency). Consider fuel cards—many offer discounts and simplify IFTA recordkeeping. Drive at moderate speeds; higher speeds reduce MPG significantly. See what is IFTA.

  • MPG: Loaded vs empty, speed, terrain affect fuel economy.
  • Deadhead: Empty miles still burn fuel without revenue.
  • Maintenance: Dirty filters and low tire pressure reduce MPG.

Common Questions

How much does fuel cost for hotshot?

Depends on diesel price, MPG, and miles. $0.50–$1.50+ per mile typical. Diesel one-ton towing: 8–12 MPG.

Diesel or gas for hotshot fuel cost?

Diesel typically better MPG when towing. Gas is lower upfront but higher fuel cost per mile.

What MPG does a hotshot truck get?

Diesel one-ton towing: 8–12 MPG typical. Empty: 12–18 MPG. MPG drops when loaded.

How do I calculate fuel cost per load?

Multiply loaded miles by (diesel price ÷ MPG). Add deadhead miles for total fuel cost.

Do fuel cards save money for hotshot?

Often yes. Fuel cards can offer discounts and simplify IFTA recordkeeping.

Does speed affect hotshot fuel economy?

Yes. Higher speeds reduce MPG. Slower cruising can improve fuel economy. Many operators find 60–65 mph offers a good balance of time and fuel.

Does load weight affect fuel cost?

Yes. Heavier loads reduce MPG. A loaded trailer can drop MPG by 2–4 compared to empty. Factor load weight into your fuel estimates.
