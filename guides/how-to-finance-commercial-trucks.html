How to Finance Commercial Trucks: Step-by-Step

This guide walks through the process of financing commercial trucks–from gathering documentation to closing. Whether you're financing a semi truck, dump truck, or bucket truck, the steps are similar. See truck financing guide and how commercial truck loans work for more detail.

Key Takeaways

  • Financing terms commonly range from 36-84 months
  • Strong credit businesses may qualify with little or no down payment

Quick Answer

To finance commercial trucks, gather business documentation (tax returns, P&L, bank statements), equipment details, and apply through a lender or broker. Most programs require 10–30% down; approval typically takes 24–72 hours for qualified borrowers.

Step 1: Gather Documentation

Collect business tax returns (2 years preferred), profit and loss statements, bank statements (3–6 months), driver's license, and equipment details (invoice, spec sheet, or listing). Some programs require fewer documents for smaller amounts or established relationships.

Why each document matters: Tax returns verify revenue and profitability over time. Bank statements show cash flow and reserves. P&L shows current-year performance. Equipment details let lenders value collateral and set advance rates. Missing or inconsistent documentation delays approval and can trigger declines.

Step 2: Identify Equipment

Know the make, model, year, VIN, and price. For used equipment, document mileage and condition. Lenders use this to determine advance rate and terms.

Step 3: Compare Lenders

Banks, credit unions, captive finance companies, and specialty lenders. Compare rates, terms, and requirements. Axiant Partners matches businesses with multiple options.

Lender types: Banks and credit unions often offer competitive rates for established businesses with strong credit. Captive finance (OEM programs) may bundle incentives with purchase. Specialty commercial lenders focus on equipment financing and may work with startups, credit challenges, or vocational equipment. Brokers like Axiant Partners compare multiple lenders in one application.

What to compare: APR, term length, down payment requirement, advance rate (loan-to-value), prepayment penalties, and documentation burden. A lower rate with a shorter term may not reduce total cost if the payment is unaffordable.

Common Reasons for Decline and How to Avoid Them

Insufficient revenue or cash flow, poor credit history, negative equity on trade-in, missing documentation, or equipment that doesn't meet lender specs (e.g., too old, too many miles). Mitigate by: strengthening revenue documentation, improving credit before applying, providing complete equipment details, and choosing equipment within typical lender parameters (e.g., used trucks under 10 years, under 500k miles for many programs).

Pre-Approval vs Full Application

Pre-approval establishes your credit and business profile before you select equipment. It can speed the process once you have a truck and gives you a known budget when shopping. Full application includes the specific equipment and leads to funding. Some lenders offer same-day pre-approval; full approval typically 24–72 hours for straightforward applications.

Step 4: Apply

Submit application with documentation. Simple applications can be approved in 1–3 days. Complex deals may take 1–2 weeks.

Step 5: Close

Review terms, sign documents, and fund. For dealer purchases, funding may go directly to the dealer. For private-party or auction purchases, funds may go to you or an escrow. Verify the final APR, payment, and term before signing. Ensure the title and lien are recorded correctly.

After funding: Make payments on time to build business credit. Keep maintenance records; they support resale value and future refinancing. Consider refinancing if rates drop or your credit improves significantly.

Common Questions

What credit score do I need to finance commercial trucks?

Many programs require 650+ for competitive terms. Some lenders work with 580–650 with higher down payments. Business revenue can offset weaker credit.

How much down payment is required?

Typically 10–30%. New equipment often allows 10–15%; used may require 20–30%. Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with $0 down.

Can startup businesses finance commercial trucks?

Yes. Some lenders work with startups. Expect 20–30% down and proof of contracts or revenue. Time in business under 12 months may limit options.

How long are commercial truck loan terms?

New trucks: 60–84 months. Used trucks: 24–60 months depending on age and mileage. Terms match equipment useful life.

How fast can I get commercial truck financing approved?

Simple applications: 1–3 business days. Complex deals: 1–2 weeks. Pre-approval can speed the process once equipment is selected.

Can I finance a used commercial truck?

Yes. Used truck financing is widely available. Terms are typically shorter (24–60 months) than for new equipment. Advance rates may be lower.

What documentation do I need?

Business tax returns, bank statements, P&L, driver's license, and equipment details (invoice, spec sheet, or listing). Some programs require fewer documents for smaller amounts.

What do commercial trucks typically cost?

Semi trucks: $120,000–$200,000. Dump trucks: $80,000–$180,000. Bucket trucks: $90,000–$250,000. Vac trucks: $150,000–$350,000.

What is the first step to finance a commercial truck?

Gather business documentation and equipment details. Compare programs from multiple lenders. Pre-approval can speed the process.
