Definition

Commercial vehicle registration in Texas involves IRP (International Registration Plan) for apportioned registration, IFTA (International Fuel Tax Agreement) for fuel tax, and state weight fees. Texas is a common base state for trucking due to its size and freight volume. Base state is where the vehicle is primarily garaged. See commercial vehicle registration by state.

What Is IRP and IFTA?

IRP (International Registration Plan) apportions registration fees across states based on miles traveled in each. IFTA (International Fuel Tax Agreement) does the same for fuel taxes. Texas participates in both. If your vehicle is 26,001+ lbs GVWR and you operate interstate, you typically need IRP and IFTA. Base state is where the vehicle is primarily garaged—often Texas for operators based there. See what is IFTA for fuel tax details.

IRP in Texas

If your vehicle is 26,001+ lbs GVWR and you operate interstate, you need IRP (International Registration Plan). Texas is a participating state. Register through Texas DPS if Texas is your base state (where the vehicle is garaged).

IFTA in Texas

IFTA apportions fuel tax across jurisdictions. Required for 26,001+ lbs or interstate. File quarterly through Texas if Texas is your base state. See what is IFTA.

Texas Weight Fees

Texas charges weight-based registration fees. Rates vary by vehicle weight. Check Texas DPS for current fees. Hotshot trucks under 26,001 lbs may have different requirements—they may not need IRP or IFTA. If you stay under 26,001 lbs GCWR, you might qualify for standard Texas registration instead of apportioned registration. Verify your exact weight and usage with Texas DPS.

How to Register

Apply through Texas DPS. Base state is where you garage the vehicle—if you're based in Texas, Texas is your base state for IRP and IFTA. USDOT registration is separate and done through FMCSA online. IRP and IFTA applications require proof of insurance, vehicle information, and mileage records. Texas offers online services for many registration tasks. Allow time for processing—IRP can take several weeks. See DOT registration requirements and owner operator business checklist.

Factors That Affect Texas Registration

  • Vehicle weight: Heavier vehicles typically pay more in IRP and weight fees.
  • Miles per state: IRP apportions fees by miles traveled in each jurisdiction.
  • Base state: Texas as base state means IRP/IFTA filed through Texas DPS.
  • Interstate vs intrastate: Intrastate-only may have different Texas rules.

Common Questions

How do I register a commercial vehicle in Texas?

Through Texas DPS. IRP if 26,001+ lbs and interstate. IFTA for fuel tax. Standard registration for lighter vehicles.

Does Texas require IRP for hotshot?

If GCWR 26,001+ lbs and interstate, yes. Under that may not need IRP.

What are Texas weight fees?

Texas weight fees vary by vehicle. Check Texas DPS for current rates.

When are IFTA returns due in Texas?

Quarterly. Due by the last day of the month following each quarter (e.g., Q1 due April 30). Late filing can result in penalties. Keep mileage and fuel purchase records for each quarter. See what is IFTA.

Can I use Texas as my base state if I live elsewhere?

Base state is typically where the vehicle is garaged. Using a state where you do not garage the vehicle can have compliance implications—verify with Texas DPS.

Do I need USDOT to register in Texas?

USDOT is required for interstate commerce, separate from state registration. Register USDOT through FMCSA first. State registration (IRP, IFTA, weight fees) is handled through Texas DPS.
