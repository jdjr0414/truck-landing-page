Trucking Factoring vs Financing: What's the Difference?

Trucking factoring and truck financing are often confused—they serve different purposes. Factoring sells your freight invoices for immediate cash flow. Financing buys equipment (trucks, trailers). Owner-operators often use both: factoring to cover fuel and expenses while waiting for shipper payment, financing to purchase trucks. This guide explains each and when to use them. What Is Trucking Factoring?

Factoring is selling your freight invoices (receivables) to a factor for immediate cash. You deliver a load; the shipper owes you $2,000 in 30–60 days. Instead of waiting, you sell that invoice to a factor. The factor advances you 80–97% ($1,600–$1,940) within 24–48 hours. When the shipper pays, the factor keeps their fee (typically 1–5% of invoice value) and remits the rest to you. Factoring solves cash flow—fuel, repairs, driver pay—while you wait for slow-paying shippers.

What Is Truck Financing?

Truck financing is a loan or lease to purchase equipment. You borrow money to buy a truck (or trailer); the truck secures the loan. You make monthly payments over 24–84 months. Financing gets you the truck; it doesn't fund day-to-day operations. See how commercial truck loans work.

When to Use Factoring

Use factoring when you need cash before shippers pay. Common scenarios: fuel advances, repair bills, driver payroll, or covering the gap between delivery and payment. Factoring works best when you have consistent loads and reliable shippers. Recourse factoring (you're liable if shipper doesn't pay) typically has lower fees than non-recourse. See trucking company financing for broader options.

When to Use Financing

Use financing when you need to buy a truck, trailer, or other equipment. Traditional loans, lease-purchase, and dealer financing all fall under equipment financing. You need financing to acquire assets; you need factoring (or other working capital) to operate. Many owner-operators use both: financing for the truck, factoring for cash flow.

Can You Use Both?

Yes. Factoring and financing serve different needs. A typical owner-operator might: finance a semi truck with a 72-month loan, use factoring to get immediate payment on loads, and use that cash to cover fuel, tolls, and expenses. The two don't conflict—they complement each other.

Common Questions

What is trucking factoring?

Selling freight invoices to a factor for immediate cash. Factor advances 80–97%, collects from shipper, keeps a fee.

What is truck financing?

Loan or lease to purchase equipment. You make payments; the truck secures the loan.

Can I use factoring and financing together?

Yes. Many owner-operators use factoring for cash flow and financing for equipment. They serve different purposes.

Is factoring a loan?

No. Factoring is selling an asset (your invoice). You're not borrowing; you're receiving an advance on money you're owed.
