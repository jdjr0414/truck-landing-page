Definition

A hotshot trucking equipment list covers the truck, trailer, hitch, cargo securement (chains, straps, binders, tarps), and compliance items (ELD, fire extinguisher, triangles). FMCSA cargo securement rules apply. See hotshot truck requirements and hotshot trailer requirements.

Vehicle and Trailer

  • One-ton dually pickup (Ford F-350/F-450, Ram 3500, Chevy 3500)
  • Gooseneck or fifth-wheel flatbed trailer (20–40 ft)
  • Gooseneck or fifth-wheel hitch

The one-ton dually is the standard hotshot truck because it offers the payload and towing capacity for heavy gooseneck loads. Diesel engines are preferred for long hauls due to better fuel economy when towing. Trailer length depends on your typical loads—20–25 ft for lighter freight, 35–40 ft for equipment and machinery. Deck-over vs low-profile affects load height and accessibility. See best truck for hotshot trucking and best trailer for hotshot trucking.

Cargo Securement

  • Chains (grade 70 or 80)
  • Straps (ratchet, 2" or 3")
  • Binders (chain binders, load binders)
  • Tarps (flatbed tarps, size to fit trailer)
  • Edge protectors

FMCSA cargo securement rules apply. Chains must meet working load limit requirements; grade 70 and 80 are common. Ratchet straps should be rated for the load. Edge protectors prevent straps from cutting into cargo. Tarps protect weather-sensitive freight. See DOT inspection checklist.

Compliance and Safety

  • ELD (if CDL required)—FMCSA mandates ELD for drivers operating vehicles requiring CDL
  • Fire extinguisher—DOT requires at least one properly mounted extinguisher
  • Reflective triangles—required for roadside stops
  • First aid kit—recommended for safety

Non-CDL operators may not need an ELD, but check your state rules. DOT inspections can cite missing or improper safety equipment. See DOT inspection checklist for a full list.

Equipment Checklist Before First Load

Before your first load, verify: truck and trailer are registered and insured; hitch is properly installed; you have chains, straps, binders, and tarps for the load type; fire extinguisher and triangles are in place; ELD is installed if you use a CDL. Complete a pre-trip inspection. See DOT inspection checklist.

Factors That Affect Equipment Needs

  • Load type: Heavy machinery vs light cargo affects trailer and securement choices.
  • Weather: Tarps and edge protectors for weather-sensitive freight.
  • CDL status: ELD required if CDL applies.
  • Trailer length: Longer trailers need more chains and straps per FMCSA rules.

Tips for Equipment

  • Buy quality chains and straps—FMCSA cargo securement rules apply.
  • Carry spare tarps and edge protectors for weather-sensitive loads.
  • Keep fire extinguisher and triangles accessible for inspections.
  • Verify hitch is rated for your trailer and load.

Common Questions

What equipment do I need for hotshot trucking?

One-ton pickup, gooseneck trailer, hitch, tarps, chains, straps, binders, and cargo securement. ELD if CDL.

How much does hotshot equipment cost?

New: $80,000–$150,000 for truck and trailer. Used: $40,000–$90,000. Add tarps, chains, etc.

Do I need an ELD for hotshot?

Yes, if you hold a CDL and operate a vehicle requiring CDL. Non-CDL may not need ELD.

What chain grade for hotshot?

Grade 70 or 80 transport chain. FMCSA cargo securement rules specify working load limits.

Can I use a bumper pull trailer for hotshot?

Gooseneck or fifth-wheel is standard for weight and stability. Bumper pull limits capacity.

How many tarps do I need?

Depends on trailer size and load type. Many operators carry 2–4 tarps for flexibility. Flatbed tarps come in various sizes; match to your trailer deck. Have spare tarps for damaged or lost covers.

What size trailer do I need for hotshot?

20–40 ft typical. 20–25 ft for lighter loads; 35–40 ft for equipment and machinery. Deck-over vs low-profile affects load height. See best trailer for hotshot trucking.
