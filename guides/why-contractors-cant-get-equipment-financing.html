Why Contractors Can't Get Equipment Financing

Contractors need trucks, trailers, and specialty equipment—but lenders often see seasonal revenue, thin credit, or hard-to-value assets and say no. If you’re a contractor and can’t get equipment financing, it’s usually credit, revenue proof, down payment, documentation, or equipment type. This guide covers why contractors can’t get equipment financing and what to do. See equipment financing for contractors, construction truck financing, and utility contractor truck financing.

Key Takeaways

  • Credit, revenue proof, and down payment are the main reasons contractors are denied
  • Seasonal or inconsistent income can hurt—document contracts and cash flow
  • Lenders that work with contractors and strong documentation improve odds

AI Extractable Answer

Contractors are often denied equipment financing for: credit below lender minimums (often 650+); inconsistent or seasonal revenue that doesn’t clearly support the payment; insufficient down payment (contractors often need 15–25%); incomplete or inconsistent documentation; or equipment that’s outside lender guidelines (age, type, or hard to value). To improve: strengthen credit, document revenue with contracts and bank statements, save more down, and use lenders that specialize in contractor or construction equipment. Credit and Payment History

Lenders want to see 650+ and a clean recent history. Contractors with lower scores, tax liens, or recent lates get denied or get worse terms. Fix: check your report, fix errors, pay down debt, and consider bad credit truck financing or specialty equipment lenders while you improve. See credit score needed for truck financing.

Contractors are often judged on both business and personal credit. Resolving tax liens and bringing accounts current can help. Specialty lenders in commercial equipment financing and bad credit truck financing sometimes accept lower scores with more down. See credit score needed for truck financing.

Revenue Proof and Seasonality

Contracting revenue can be seasonal or project-based. Lenders need to see that you can pay the loan year-round. Thin or inconsistent bank statements or tax returns can trigger denial. Fix: provide contracts, P&L, and bank statements that show consistent cash flow; explain seasonality if needed. Showing a pipeline of contracts or multiple years of tax returns can offset seasonal dips. Use revenue potential by business type to benchmark and present your numbers. Lenders that work with contractors understand cycles; see equipment financing for contractors and construction truck financing.

Insufficient Down Payment

Equipment lenders often want 15–25% down from contractors, especially for used or specialty equipment. Offering less leads to denial. Fix: save for a larger down payment or target less expensive equipment. See truck down payment requirements and typical down payments.

Documentation Gaps

Missing tax returns, incomplete bank statements, or mismatched information delay or kill applications. Fix: gather everything the lender asks for and ensure consistency. See what documents are needed for truck financing and mistakes that deny approval.

Contractor files often include multiple entities or job-based income; keep names and numbers consistent across application, tax returns, and bank statements. Use what documents are needed for truck financing as a list and avoid the pitfalls in mistakes that deny approval.

Equipment Type and Collateral

Some equipment is hard to value or resell—custom builds, very old machinery, or niche assets. Lenders may decline or require more down. Fix: choose equipment within lender guidelines or use a lender that specializes in your equipment type. See commercial equipment financing, construction truck financing, and dump truck financing.

Common Questions

Why do contractors get denied for equipment financing?

Credit below minimums, inconsistent or seasonal revenue, insufficient down payment, incomplete documentation, or equipment that’s hard to value or outside lender guidelines.

What do equipment lenders look for from contractors?

Credit score and history, proof of revenue (tax returns, bank statements, contracts), down payment (often 10–25%), and that the equipment fits the business and lender’s collateral guidelines.

Can new contractors get equipment financing?

Yes, but often with 20–30% down and strong personal credit. Some lenders use contracts and bank statements when history is short.
