Commercial Truck Insurance Cost (2026 Guide)

Commercial truck insurance typically costs $4,000–$15,000 per year depending on truck type, coverage limits, driver history, and cargo. This guide covers liability, cargo, physical damage, and typical costs by vehicle type.

Definition

Commercial truck insurance covers liability (bodily injury and property damage to others), cargo (freight in transit), and physical damage (collision and comprehensive). Federal law requires primary liability for interstate commerce; brokers and lenders often require additional coverage.

Types of Coverage

Primary liability: Required for interstate commerce. Covers bodily injury and property damage to others. Minimum federal: $750k; brokers often require $1M+.

Cargo insurance: Covers freight in transit. Required for for-hire hauling. Typical limits: $100k–$250k.

Physical damage: Collision and comprehensive. Lenders require this if the truck is financed.

Coverage Limits and Cost

Federal minimum liability: $750,000. Most brokers require $1,000,000 or higher. Cargo limits typically $100,000–$250,000. Physical damage is based on equipment value. Higher limits increase premium but may be required for certain loads.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Coverage limits: Higher limits = higher premium.
  • Driver history: Accidents and violations increase cost.
  • Operating radius: Long-haul often costs more than local.
  • Cargo type: Hazmat and high-value cargo increase cost.
  • Vehicle age and value: Physical damage tied to equipment value.
  • Business age: New businesses may pay more until they establish a safety record.

Common Industries

Commercial truck insurance applies to trucking companies, owner-operators, construction contractors, utility contractors, waste haulers, and delivery businesses. Each industry may have specific coverage requirements from customers or contracts.

Startup Considerations

New businesses may pay more until they establish a safety record. Some insurers require 6–12 months in business. Brokers and shippers require proof of insurance before loading. Common Questions

How much does commercial truck insurance cost?

Typical range: $4,000–$15,000 per year depending on truck type, coverage limits, and driver history. Semi trucks and hotshot often $6k–$12k.

What insurance do I need for a commercial truck?

Primary liability (required), cargo insurance (for-hire), physical damage (if financed). Brokers often require $1M+ liability.

Is commercial truck insurance required?

Primary liability is required for interstate commerce. Cargo and physical damage are often required by lenders and brokers.

Do brokers require specific insurance limits?

Yes. Most freight brokers require $1,000,000+ primary liability and cargo insurance. Proof of insurance must be on file before loading.

Can new businesses get commercial truck insurance?

Yes, but some insurers require 6–12 months in business. New businesses may pay higher premiums until they establish a safety record.

Is physical damage required if I finance my truck?

Yes. Lenders require physical damage (collision and comprehensive) to protect their collateral when the truck is financed.
