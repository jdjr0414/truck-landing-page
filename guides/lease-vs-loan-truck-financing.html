Lease vs Loan Truck Financing: Compare Your Options

Commercial truck financing can be structured as a loan (you own the truck) or a lease (you use it for a term). Loans build equity; leases often have lower monthly payments and different tax treatment. This guide compares both options so you can choose the right structure for your business.

Quick Answer

Loan: You own the truck; build equity; payments until paid off. Lease: You use the truck for a term; often lower payments; no ownership unless buyout. High-mileage users typically prefer loans. Lease may suit fleets that cycle equipment.

When to Choose a Loan

  • You want to own the truck long-term
  • High mileage (OTR, freight, heavy use)
  • You plan to keep the truck past the loan term
  • You want to build equity and have an asset

When to Choose a Lease

  • Lower monthly payments are a priority
  • You prefer to cycle equipment every few years
  • Predictable mileage (lease terms often include caps)
  • Tax structure favors lease deductions

Common Questions

What is the difference between leasing and financing a truck?

Loan: you own the truck; make payments until paid off. Lease: you use the truck for a term; typically no ownership at end unless buyout. Lease payments may be lower; loan builds equity.

Is it better to lease or finance a commercial truck?

Depends on goals. Lease: lower payments, no residual risk. Loan: ownership, equity, no mileage caps. High-mileage users often prefer loans.

Can I deduct truck lease payments on taxes?

Yes. Commercial truck lease payments are typically deductible as a business expense. Consult a tax professional.

Do I need good credit for a truck lease?

Yes. Lease approval typically requires similar credit to a loan—650+ for competitive terms. Lessors assess credit and business revenue.

Can startups lease commercial trucks?

Some lessors work with startups. Expect similar requirements to loans—proof of revenue, 20–30% down or equivalent, strong personal credit.
