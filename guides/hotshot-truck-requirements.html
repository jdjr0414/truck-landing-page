Definition

A hotshot truck is a one-ton or larger pickup truck (typically dually) paired with a gooseneck or fifth-wheel flatbed trailer, used to haul time-sensitive freight such as equipment, machinery parts, oilfield supplies, and manufactured goods. Hotshot operators often run under 26,000 lbs GCWR to avoid CDL requirements in certain states. See what is hot shot trucking.

Vehicle Requirements

Hotshot trucking typically uses a one-ton pickup truck (dually) with a gooseneck or fifth-wheel flatbed trailer. Common trucks: Ford F-350/F-450, Ram 3500, Chevrolet 3500. The chassis must meet GVWR and towing capacity for your target loads. Single rear-wheel pickups can work for lighter loads under 15,000 lbs, but dually configurations provide better stability and payload capacity for most commercial hotshot work.

Typical Hotshot Truck Specs

  • One-ton dually pickup (Class 2–3)
  • Gooseneck or fifth-wheel hitch
  • GVWR: 10,000–14,000 lbs (truck)
  • GCWR: 26,000–30,000+ lbs (truck + trailer + cargo)
  • Trailer: 20–40 ft flatbed, 14,000–25,000 lb GVWR

Typical Equipment Cost

Hotshot equipment costs vary by truck make, trailer size, and condition. New setups typically run $80,000–$150,000; used 3–5 year old rigs often sell for $40,000–$90,000. See hotshot trucking startup cost for a full breakdown.

GVWR and CDL Requirements

Federal CDL rules: combination GVWR over 26,001 lbs with trailer over 10,000 lbs requires Class A CDL. Many hotshot operators stay under 26,001 lbs GCWR to avoid CDL in certain states. See CDL vs non-CDL hotshot for details. State rules vary—verify with your state DMV. The GVWR (Gross Vehicle Weight Rating) is the maximum weight the vehicle is designed to carry; GCWR applies to the combined truck, trailer, and cargo.

Trailer Requirements

Gooseneck or fifth-wheel flatbed trailers are standard. Length: 20–40 ft. GVWR: 14,000–25,000 lbs. Must have proper tie-downs, tarps, and chains for cargo securement. Match trailer capacity to truck GCWR. Deck-over vs low-profile designs affect load height and accessibility. Aluminum trailers weigh less and improve payload; steel is more durable and often lower cost. See best truck for hotshot trucking for truck and trailer pairing guidance.

Revenue Potential

Hotshot trucking businesses typically generate $150,000–$600,000+ in annual revenue depending on location, freight mix, and utilization. Oilfield and expedited freight often command $2–$4+ per mile. See revenue potential by business type for comparison with other commercial vehicle businesses.

DOT and Authority Requirements

USDOT number required for interstate commerce. FMCSA operating authority (MC number) required for for-hire hauling. See DOT registration requirements and DOT number for trucking business. Intrastate-only operations may have different rules depending on state.

Typical License and Authority Requirements

  • USDOT number (interstate)
  • MC authority (for-hire hauling)
  • Class A CDL (if GCWR exceeds 26,001 lbs)
  • State business registration
  • IRP/IFTA (if 26,001+ lbs or interstate)

Common Industries

Hotshot operators serve oilfield contractors, equipment dealers, manufacturers, construction companies, and freight brokers. Time-sensitive and oversize loads are common. Building relationships with dispatchers and project managers drives steady work.

Common Questions

What GVWR do I need for hotshot trucking?

Stay under 26,001 lbs GVWR to avoid CDL in many states. One-ton pickup + gooseneck: typically 26,000 lbs or less. Check GCWR for combination weight.

Do I need a CDL for hotshot trucking?

If GVWR under 26,001 lbs and trailer under 10,000 lbs, you may not need a CDL in some states. Over those thresholds requires Class A CDL. See CDL vs non-CDL hotshot.

What truck is best for hotshot?

One-ton dually pickup: Ford F-350/F-450, Ram 3500, Chevrolet 3500. Need gooseneck or fifth-wheel hitch. See best truck for hotshot trucking.

What trailer do I need for hotshot?

Gooseneck or fifth-wheel flatbed trailer. 20–40 ft common. 14,000–25,000 lb GVWR typical. Must match truck GCWR.

How much does hotshot equipment cost?

New: $80,000–$150,000 for truck and trailer. Used: $40,000–$90,000. See hotshot trucking startup cost for full breakdown.

Can I finance hotshot equipment as a new business?

Yes. Some lenders work with new hotshot businesses. Down payment typically 20–30%; strong credit may qualify for less. Proof of contracts helps.

What revenue can I expect from hotshot trucking?

Typical annual revenue: $150,000–$600,000+ depending on location, freight mix, and utilization. Oilfield work often pays $2–$4+ per mile.

Do I need a dually for hotshot?

Dually is recommended for stability with heavy gooseneck loads. Single rear wheel can work for lighter loads under 15,000 lbs.
