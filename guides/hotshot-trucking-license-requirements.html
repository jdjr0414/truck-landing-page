Hotshot Trucking License Requirements (2026 Guide)

Hotshot trucking license requirements include CDL (or not), USDOT number, MC authority, and state registration. This guide covers what you need to run legally. See CDL vs non-CDL hotshot and DOT registration requirements.

  • USDOT required for interstate commerce
  • MC authority for for-hire hauling
  • State rules vary—verify with DMV

Definition

Hotshot trucking license requirements include federal and state rules for driver credentials, carrier registration (USDOT), and operating authority (MC). Whether you need a CDL depends on your truck and trailer weight. See CDL vs non-CDL hotshot and commercial truck license requirements.

CDL Requirements

Federal rule: combination GVWR over 26,001 lbs with trailer over 10,000 lbs requires Class A CDL. Many hotshot rigs exceed these limits—a one-ton dually with a 20,000 lb gooseneck trailer often pushes past 26,001 lbs GCWR. If you want to run without a CDL, you must stay under both the 26,001 lb combination limit and the 10,000 lb trailer limit. State rules can be stricter than federal; always verify with your state DMV. Some states have different thresholds for intrastate-only operations. See CDL vs non-CDL hotshot and commercial truck license requirements.

USDOT Number

Required for interstate commerce. Register through FMCSA online. No fee. Registration is same-day. You'll need your business information and vehicle details. USDOT is used for safety monitoring and must be displayed on your vehicle. Brokers and shippers verify your USDOT status. See DOT number for trucking business.

MC Authority

Required for for-hire hauling. MC (Motor Carrier) authority in addition to USDOT. See what is an MC number. Process takes 2–4 weeks; requires insurance filing. You cannot haul for hire without MC authority. Brokers and shippers verify your authority status before assigning loads.

Other Licenses

Medical certificate (if CDL). State business registration. IRP/IFTA if 26,001+ lbs and interstate. Some states require additional endorsements. Verify with your state DMV. See how to start a hotshot trucking business.

Factors That Affect License Requirements

  • Vehicle weight: GCWR and trailer GVWR determine CDL need.
  • Interstate vs intrastate: USDOT and MC apply to interstate for-hire.
  • For-hire vs private: MC authority for for-hire hauling.
  • State rules: Some states have stricter rules than federal.

Tips for Getting Licensed

  • Register USDOT first—it's free and same-day.
  • Apply for MC authority early—allow 2–4 weeks; requires insurance filing.
  • If you need a CDL, budget time for training and testing.
  • Verify your truck and trailer weights—they determine CDL requirement.
  • Keep copies of all authority and registration documents handy.

Bottom Line

Hotshot license requirements center on three things: CDL (if 26,001+ lbs GCWR and trailer 10,001+ lbs), USDOT (for interstate), and MC authority (for for-hire). Get USDOT first, then MC with insurance. Verify your exact weights—they determine whether you need a CDL. State rules can differ from federal. Allow 2–4 weeks for MC processing. Medical certificate required if CDL applies.

Common Questions

What license do I need for hotshot trucking?

CDL if GCWR over 26,001 lbs and trailer over 10,000 lbs. Otherwise may not need CDL in some states. USDOT for interstate.

Do I need MC authority for hotshot?

Yes, for for-hire hauling. MC (Motor Carrier) authority is required in addition to USDOT.

What is the 26,001 lb rule for hotshot?

Federal: combination over 26,001 lbs with trailer over 10,000 lbs requires Class A CDL.

Can I run hotshot without a CDL?

Yes, if you stay under 26,001 lbs GCWR and trailer under 10,001 lbs. Many hotshot rigs exceed this—verify your weights.

Do I need USDOT for intrastate hotshot?

Some states require USDOT for intrastate. Check your state's rules.

How long does MC authority take?

Typically 2–4 weeks. Requires insurance filing and processing by FMCSA. You must have active insurance before authority is granted. Processing time can vary during busy periods.

What if I already have a CDL from another state?

CDLs are generally valid across states. You may need to transfer your CDL if you move. Medical certificate must be current. Verify with your state DMV.
