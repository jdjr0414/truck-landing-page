Definition

A hotshot truck is a one-ton or heavier pickup (typically dually) equipped with a gooseneck or fifth-wheel hitch to tow flatbed trailers. Hotshot trucking hauls time-sensitive freight, often machinery, equipment, or materials. The best truck matches your typical load weight and GCWR to avoid CDL if desired. What to Choose

Dually vs single rear wheel: Dually recommended for stability with heavy gooseneck loads. Single rear wheel can work for lighter loads under 15,000 lbs.

Diesel vs gas: Diesel offers better fuel economy and torque for long hauls. Gas is lower upfront cost. Many hotshot operators prefer diesel.

GVWR and CDL: Stay under 26,001 lbs GCWR to avoid CDL in some states. See CDL vs non-CDL hotshot.

Trailer Match

Gooseneck or fifth-wheel flatbed trailers. 20–40 ft common. 14,000–25,000 lb GVWR. Match trailer GVWR to truck GCWR. See hotshot truck requirements.

Common Questions

What is the best truck for hotshot trucking?

One-ton dually: Ford F-350/F-450, Ram 3500, Chevrolet 3500. Need gooseneck/fifth-wheel hitch. GVWR and towing capacity must match your loads.

Do I need a dually for hotshot?

Dually (dual rear wheels) is recommended for stability with heavy gooseneck loads. Single rear wheel can work for lighter loads under 15,000 lbs.

How much can a hotshot truck tow?

One-ton pickups typically tow 18,000–24,000 lbs with gooseneck. GCWR limits total combination weight. Stay under 26,001 lbs to avoid CDL in some states.

Diesel or gas for hotshot?

Diesel offers better fuel economy and torque for long hauls. Gas is lower upfront cost. Many hotshot operators prefer diesel for heavy loads.

How much does a hotshot truck and trailer cost?

New one-ton dually: $55,000–$85,000. Gooseneck trailer: $15,000–$45,000. Total new setup often $70,000–$130,000. See hotshot trucking startup cost.

What trailer size for hotshot?

20–40 ft gooseneck flatbed common. 14,000–25,000 lb GVWR. Match trailer to truck GCWR and typical load.
