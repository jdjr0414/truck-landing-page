Diesel vs Gas for Hotshot Dually

Diesel is the standard for hotshot dually trucks. Better MPG when towing, longer engine life under heavy use, and higher resale value. Gas costs less upfront but burns more fuel per mile when loaded. For full-time hotshot, diesel typically pays off within a few years. Def system maintenance is a consideration for newer diesels. Pre-purchase inspection recommended for used trucks. Check frame and suspension for damage or wear.

Why Dually?

Dual rear wheels improve stability with heavy gooseneck loads. Better weight distribution, less sway in crosswinds and when passing. Single rear wheel can work for lighter loads under 15,000 lbs, but many operators prefer dually for the margin of safety and capacity headroom. Gooseneck and fifth-wheel hitches place significant weight over the rear axle; dually tires spread that load. Tire replacement costs more for dually (six rear tires vs two). Factors That Affect Dually Choice

  • Load weight: Heavier loads favor dually for stability.
  • Trailer type: Gooseneck and fifth-wheel put weight over rear axle.
  • Diesel vs gas: Diesel preferred for long hauls and towing.
  • CDL status: Over 26,001 lbs GCWR may require CDL. See CDL vs non-CDL hotshot.

Tips for Choosing a Dually

  • Verify towing capacity for your trailer and typical loads.
  • Diesel preferred for long hauls—better MPG when towing.
  • Check payload and GCWR—they affect CDL requirement.
  • Compare used vs new—used can save $20,000+.
  • Check payload sticker—must handle trailer tongue weight.
