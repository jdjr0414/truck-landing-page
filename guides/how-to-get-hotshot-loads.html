Definition

Getting hotshot loads means finding freight to haul using load boards, freight brokers, direct shippers, or dispatch services. Hotshot freight is typically time-sensitive, flatbed, or oversize. See hotshot trucking profit per load.

Load Boards

Load boards list available freight. Filter by flatbed, gooseneck, trailer type, weight, and lane. DAT, Truckstop.com, 123Loadboard are major options. $50–$150/month typical. See load boards comparison below.

Freight Brokers

Brokers connect shippers with carriers. Register with brokers; they often require MC authority, insurance ($1M+ liability), and cargo. Building relationships with a few brokers can yield steady loads. Brokers post loads on load boards but also work with preferred carriers directly. Get on a broker's preferred list by delivering on time, communicating clearly, and maintaining a clean safety record. Brokers take a margin—typically 10–20%—but they handle the shipper relationship. Multiple broker relationships diversify your load source. Be professional and responsive in all communications.

Direct Shippers

Manufacturers, equipment dealers, oilfield suppliers, and construction companies often need hotshot. Contact directly—introduce your service, provide MC and insurance details, and ask about their freight needs. Steady lanes often pay better than spot market because you remove the broker margin. Building relationships takes time but can yield consistent loads and better rates. Focus on industries in your region: oilfield in Texas and the Dakotas, equipment dealers nationwide, construction in growing markets. Follow up regularly to stay top of mind.

Building Your Load Network

Start with load boards and 2–3 brokers. Add direct shipper contacts as you identify opportunities. It takes weeks to months to build a solid network. Consistency—reliable service, clear communication—builds trust. Diversify so you're not dependent on one source. Patience and persistence pay off. Track which sources yield the best loads and rates over time.

Dispatch Services

Dispatch services find loads for a fee or percentage of revenue. Useful for new operators or those who prefer not to hunt loads themselves. They handle broker communication and load booking. Fees vary—some charge a flat monthly rate, others take a percentage per load. Evaluate whether the cost is worth the time you save. Some operators use dispatch when starting out and transition to finding loads themselves once they learn the lanes.

Factors That Affect Finding Loads

  • Region: Oilfield and manufacturing hubs have more hotshot freight.
  • Season: Construction and agriculture peak in certain months.
  • Credentials: MC authority, insurance ($1M+), cargo. Brokers verify.
  • Response time: Quick response to load posts improves booking.

Tips for Finding Loads

  • Get on broker preferred lists—deliver on time, communicate clearly.
  • Contact direct shippers in your region—oilfield, equipment, manufacturing.
  • Use load boards for spot market while building relationships.
  • Keep MC, insurance, and cargo current—brokers verify.
  • Respond quickly when brokers or shippers contact you.

Common Questions

How do I find hotshot loads?

Load boards (DAT, Truckstop.com), freight brokers, direct shippers, dispatch services. Build relationships.

Do I need a load board for hotshot?

No. Load boards are one option. Direct shippers and brokers are alternatives.

What industries need hotshot freight?

Oilfield, equipment dealers, manufacturers, construction, agriculture. Time-sensitive and oversize loads.

How do I get loads as a new hotshot operator?

Start with load boards and brokers. Build relationships. Consider dispatch services while you learn the lanes.

What do brokers need to give me loads?

MC authority, $1M+ liability insurance, cargo insurance. Some require minimum operating time.

Should I use multiple load boards?

Many operators use 2–3 boards to maximize load options. Compare fees and load volume. Some boards have overlapping loads; others have unique postings.

How long does it take to build load relationships?

Weeks to months. Brokers may work with you quickly if you have authority and insurance. Direct shippers often take longer—multiple contacts and consistent service build trust.

How much do load boards cost?

$50–$150/month typical. Some offer free trials or pay-per-load options.

Which load board has the most flatbed loads?

DAT and Truckstop.com typically have high flatbed volume.

What trailer types should I filter for hotshot?

Flatbed, gooseneck, flatbed with gooseneck. Some boards use "hotshot" or "expedited" as filters.
