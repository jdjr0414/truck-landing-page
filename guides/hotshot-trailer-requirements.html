Definition

A hotshot trailer is a gooseneck or fifth-wheel flatbed trailer designed to be towed by a one-ton or larger pickup truck. Hotshot trailers haul time-sensitive freight such as equipment, machinery, and manufactured goods. Requirements include proper GVWR, cargo securement per FMCSA, and matching the trailer to the truck's GCWR. See what is hot shot trucking.

Trailer Type

Gooseneck or fifth-wheel flatbed trailers are standard for hotshot. Both connect to a hitch in the truck bed. Gooseneck is more common for commercial hotshot; fifth-wheel offers similar capacity. Bumper-pull trailers are not suitable for heavy hotshot loads.

GVWR and CDL

Trailer GVWR over 10,000 lbs combined with truck+trailer+cargo over 26,001 lbs GCWR requires Class A CDL. To run non-CDL, trailer must stay under 10,000 lbs GVWR—which limits payload. See CDL vs non-CDL hotshot.

Cargo Securement

FMCSA cargo securement rules apply. Chains, straps, binders, and tarps. No shifting or loose cargo. See DOT inspection checklist. Aluminum vs steel: aluminum weighs less (more payload); steel is durable and often lower cost.

Deck-Over vs Low-Profile

Deck-over: Deck sits above the wheels. Higher load surface, easier loading of tall equipment. Low-profile: Deck between or behind wheels. Lower center of gravity, better for some loads. Choose based on typical freight. See best trailer for hotshot trucking.

Revenue Potential

Trailer choice affects payload and versatility. Larger trailers handle more freight but may require CDL. See revenue potential by business type and hotshot trucking profit per load.

Common Industries

Hotshot trailers serve oilfield contractors, equipment dealers, manufacturers, construction companies, and freight brokers. Machinery, equipment parts, and time-sensitive loads are common.

Factors That Affect Trailer Requirements

  • Load type: Oversize or heavy loads need higher GVWR and proper axles.
  • CDL preference: Trailer under 10,000 lbs GVWR may avoid CDL in some states.
  • Material: Aluminum weighs less (more payload); steel is durable and lower cost.
  • Truck GCWR: Trailer must not exceed truck's combined capacity.

Common Questions

What trailer do I need for hotshot?

Gooseneck or fifth-wheel flatbed. 20–40 ft. 14,000–25,000 lb GVWR. Must match truck GCWR.

Gooseneck vs fifth-wheel for hotshot?

Both work. Gooseneck is more common for commercial hotshot. Fifth-wheel offers similar capacity.

What GVWR trailer for non-CDL hotshot?

Stay under 10,000 lbs trailer GVWR to avoid CDL in many states. Limits payload significantly.

Aluminum or steel trailer for hotshot?

Aluminum: lighter, more payload, higher cost. Steel: durable, lower cost, heavier. See best trailer for hotshot trucking.

How much does a hotshot trailer cost?

New: $15,000–$45,000 depending on size and material. Used: $8,000–$25,000. See hotshot trucking startup cost.

What cargo securement do I need?

Chains (grade 70 or 80), straps, binders, tarps. FMCSA cargo securement rules apply. See DOT inspection checklist.
