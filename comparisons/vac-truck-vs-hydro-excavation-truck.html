Vac Truck vs Hydro Excavation Truck

Vac trucks and hydro excavation trucks both use vacuum systems, but for different applications. Vac trucks handle industrial vacuum, septic hauling, and general excavation. Hydro excavation trucks (hydrovac) add high-pressure water for non-destructive digging around utilities. This comparison covers costs, industries, and financing.

Key Differences

Vac trucks use a vacuum system to remove material–dry or wet. Applications include septic hauling, industrial vacuum, catch basin cleaning, and general excavation. Tank capacity and vacuum specs drive value. Used across environmental services, septic, oilfield, and industrial cleaning.

Hydro excavation trucks (hydrovac) combine high-pressure water to break up soil with a vacuum to remove material. This non-destructive method exposes underground utilities without damaging pipes or cables. Required for many utility locate and daylighting projects. Higher acquisition cost due to water system, vacuum, and debris tank.

Use-Case Recommendations

  • Septic or portable toilet hauler: Vac truck. No water system needed; vacuum-only is sufficient.
  • Utility locate or daylighting contractor: Hydro excavation truck. Water + vacuum is required for non-destructive digging around buried utilities.
  • Industrial cleaning or oilfield support: Vac truck. Tank capacity and vacuum specs matter most.
  • Environmental services with mixed work: Some operators run both—vac for hauling, hydrovac for locate work.

Cost and Benefit Summary

Hydro excavation trucks cost more ($200k–$450k new) due to the water system, vacuum, and debris tank. Vac trucks ($150k–$350k) are lower cost but cannot perform hydrovac work. Hydrovac is often required by utilities and municipalities for locate projects. Both have strong financing availability and resale markets.

Which Is Right for You?

Choose a vac truck if you haul septic, perform industrial vacuum, or need general excavation without the water system. Choose a hydro excavation truck if you perform utility locate work, daylighting, or non-destructive digging around buried lines. Hydrovac is often required by utilities and municipalities. Both are high-value vocational equipment with strong financing availability.
