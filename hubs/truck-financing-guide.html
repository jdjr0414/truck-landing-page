How to Finance Commercial Trucks: Complete Guide

Commercial truck financing is a broad category covering semi trucks, dump trucks, bucket trucks, box trucks, and dozens of vocational and specialty vehicles. This guide explains how truck loans work, what lenders look for, and how to structure financing for new and used equipment.

How Commercial Truck Loans Work

Commercial truck loans are loans secured by the truck. The lender holds a lien on the title until the loan is paid. If you default, the lender can repossess the truck. Payments are typically fixed monthly. Interest rates depend on credit, equipment type, age, and term length. Most lenders require the truck to be used primarily for business purposes.

Types of Trucks Financed

Lenders finance a wide range of truck types. Each has different valuation considerations.

Over-the-Road Trucks

Semi truck financing, day cab financing, and sleeper truck financing cover tractors for freight. Heavy-haul truck financing covers specialized hauling. These units have strong resale markets.

Construction and Hauling

Dump truck financing, cement truck financing, flatbed truck financing, and logging truck financing serve construction and materials hauling. Tanker truck financing covers liquid hauling.

Vocational Trucks

Bucket truck financing, vac truck financing, service truck financing, and utility truck financing support utilities, tree care, telecom, and field service.

Delivery and Logistics

Box truck financing, refrigerated truck financing, and tow truck financing support delivery, cold chain, and roadside service.

Down Payment Requirements

Down payments typically range from 10% to 30%. New trucks from dealers often allow 10–15% for qualified borrowers. Used trucks, especially those over 5 years old, may require 20–30%. Advance rates—the percentage of value financed—usually cap at 80–90% for new and 70–80% for used. Strong credit and revenue can reduce requirements.

New vs. Used Truck Financing

New trucks qualify for longer terms (60–84 months), lower rates, and higher advance rates. Manufacturer programs and captive lenders offer promotional rates. Used truck financing is more restrictive: shorter terms (24–60 months), higher rates, and lower advances. Lenders consider remaining useful life and mileage. A 3-year-old truck with 300,000 miles will have different terms than a 2-year-old truck with 150,000 miles.

What Lenders Evaluate

  • Time in business: Most prefer 12–24 months minimum; stronger programs require 2+ years.
  • Revenue: Annual revenue and cash flow support the payment.
  • Credit: Personal and business credit affect rate and approval.
  • Equipment: Age, mileage, condition, and resale value.

Fleet Financing

Fleet acquisitions—multiple trucks or replacement cycles—often use dedicated programs. Lenders may offer volume pricing, streamlined documentation, and master agreements. See commercial vehicle financing for fleet structures.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do commercial truck loans work?

Commercial truck loans are secured by the truck. Lenders evaluate business credit, revenue, and equipment value. Terms typically run 24–84 months with fixed monthly payments.

What is the typical down payment for a truck?

Down payments typically range from 10% to 30%. New trucks often allow 10–15%; used trucks may require 20–30%.

Can I finance a truck with bad credit?

Some lenders work with lower credit scores. Expect higher down payments or shorter terms. Specialty lenders often have more flexibility.

How long can I finance a commercial truck?

New trucks: 60–84 months. Used trucks: 24–60 months depending on age and mileage.

What documents do I need for truck financing?

Business tax returns, bank statements, P&L, driver's license, and equipment details (invoice or listing).
