How to Finance Commercial Trucks: Complete Guide

Commercial truck and vehicle financing covers semi trucks, dump trucks, bucket trucks, vac trucks, box trucks, trailers, and dozens of vocational and specialty vehicles. This guide explains how truck loans work, what lenders look for, down payment and credit requirements, and how to structure financing for new and used equipment. For machinery and heavy equipment (excavators, loaders, etc.), see Commercial Equipment Financing.

How Commercial Truck Loans Work

Commercial truck loans are loans secured by the truck. The lender holds a lien on the title until the loan is paid. If you default, the lender can repossess the truck. Payments are typically fixed monthly. Interest rates depend on credit, equipment type, age, and term length. Most lenders require the truck to be used primarily for business purposes.

Types of Trucks Financed

Lenders finance a wide range of truck types. Each has different valuation considerations.

Over-the-Road Trucks

Semi truck financing, day cab financing, and sleeper truck financing cover tractors for freight. Heavy-haul truck financing covers specialized hauling. These units have strong resale markets.

Construction and Hauling

Dump truck financing, cement truck financing, flatbed truck financing, and logging truck financing serve construction and materials hauling. Tanker truck financing covers liquid hauling.

Vocational Trucks

Bucket truck financing, vac truck financing, service truck financing, and utility truck financing support utilities, tree care, telecom, and field service.

Delivery and Logistics

Box truck financing, refrigerated truck financing, and tow truck financing support delivery, cold chain, and roadside service.

Typical Financing Scenarios

Financing terms vary by borrower profile. Companies with strong credit and established revenue often qualify with little or no down payment, competitive rates, and longer terms. Higher-risk scenarios–such as startups, owner-operators without load history, or businesses rebuilding credit–may require larger down payments (20–30%), shorter terms, or higher rates. Lenders assess each application individually.

  • Established trucking companies: Fleets with 2+ years in business and steady revenue typically receive the best terms–often 10–15% down or less and 60–84 month terms.
  • Owner-operators: Solo drivers may qualify with proof of carrier agreements or load history. Down payments of 15–25% are common; first-time buyers may need 20–30%.
  • Startups: New businesses often need 20–30% down, a business plan, and proof of contracts. Time in business under 12 months may limit options.
  • Companies with strong credit: 720+ FICO and established revenue may qualify with $0 down and the most favorable rates and terms.
  • Companies rebuilding credit: Lower scores (580–650) may still qualify through specialty lenders with 15–25% down. Consistent revenue helps.

New vs. Used Truck Financing

New trucks qualify for longer terms (60–84 months), lower rates, and higher advance rates. Manufacturer programs and captive lenders offer promotional rates. Used truck financing is more restrictive: shorter terms (24–60 months), higher rates, and lower advances. Lenders consider remaining useful life and mileage. A 3-year-old truck with 300,000 miles will have different terms than a 2-year-old truck with 150,000 miles.

What Lenders Evaluate

  • Time in business: Most prefer 12–24 months minimum; stronger programs require 2+ years.
  • Revenue: Annual revenue and cash flow support the payment.
  • Credit: Personal and business credit affect rate and approval.
  • Equipment: Age, mileage, condition, and resale value.

Fleet and Multi-Unit Financing

Fleet acquisitions–multiple trucks or replacement cycles–often use dedicated programs. Lenders may offer volume pricing, streamlined documentation, and master agreements. Fleet programs support construction, freight, utilities, and waste haulers. See commercial fleet financing guide for structures.

Trailers and Specialty Equipment

Trailers (flatbed, reefer, dry van, lowboy) and specialty equipment (cranes, bodies, refrigeration units) can be financed separately or with the power unit. Lenders assess resale value and useful life. Fire truck financing, garbage truck financing, and crane truck financing follow similar structures with equipment-specific valuation. See specialty commercial vehicle financing for niche equipment.

Tax and Refinancing

Section 179 and bonus depreciation can reduce taxable income for equipment purchases. Consult a tax advisor for current limits. Existing truck loans can often be refinanced when rates drop or credit improves. Refinancing may lower payments, extend terms, or free up cash. Restructuring is an option when cash flow is tight–discuss forbearance or modification with your lender before missing payments.

Who Offers Truck Financing?

Banks, credit unions, captive finance companies (OEM-affiliated), and specialty commercial lenders. Brokers and marketplaces connect borrowers with multiple lenders. Axiant Partners matches businesses with truck financing options based on equipment type and credit profile.

Common Questions

What credit score is needed for truck financing?

Many lenders prefer 600+ for competitive rates. 720+ typically qualifies for best terms. Some specialty lenders work with 550–600.

How much down payment is required for commercial trucks?

Typically 10–30%. Strong credit and established businesses may qualify with $0 down. Down payments are risk-based, not mandatory.

Can startup trucking companies get financing?

Yes. Some lenders work with startups. Expect 20–30% down and proof of contracts or load history. Time in business under 12 months may limit options.

How long are truck loan terms?

New trucks: 60–84 months. Used trucks: 24–60 months depending on age and mileage. Terms are tied to equipment useful life.

How quickly can truck financing be approved?

Pre-approval: same-day to 48 hours. Full approval and funding: typically 1–5 business days.

Can I finance a used commercial truck?

Yes. Used truck financing is widely available. Terms are typically shorter (24–60 months) than for new equipment, with lower advance rates.

What documents do I need for truck financing?

Business tax returns, bank statements, P&L, driver's license, and equipment details (invoice or listing).

How much does a commercial truck typically cost?

Semi trucks: $120,000–$200,000. Dump trucks: $80,000–$180,000. Bucket trucks: $90,000–$250,000. Vac trucks: $150,000–$350,000.

What types of trucks can I finance?

Semi trucks, dump trucks, bucket trucks, vac trucks, box trucks, tow trucks, service trucks, fire trucks, garbage trucks, and dozens of vocational and specialty vehicles. Trailers can be financed separately or with the power unit.

Can I finance multiple trucks at once?

Yes. Fleet financing programs support multi-unit acquisitions. Lenders may offer volume pricing, streamlined documentation, and master agreements.
